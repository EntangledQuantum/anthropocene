## Teaching sequence and boundary

Compare a narrow tall triangle with a lower broad trapezoid; reshape before naming the integral. Then balance opposite lobes, sketch K rather than copying F, and reorder equal-area lobes for a low-energy cart. Three Predicts are competing interpretations of the visible force world; one SketchCurve is a representation shift. Two Recall cards use named front slots. Twelve minutes includes choosing, reshaping, inspecting, drawing and retrying rather than twelve minutes of prose.

The five bounded handles give continuous piecewise-linear F(x), no spline overshoot. Knots and zero crossings are included in trapezoidal integration, making signed areas exact for this family. Positive and negative accounts use cumulativeWork imported from work.ts. Independently measured K uses work.ts pushThroughField (velocity Verlet), not K=K0+W. The spatial inspector clamps to the first forward-passage endpoint. An analytic sign-interval root brackets the earliest K=0 and prevents a later allowed island from appearing as reachable. Root-location refines the coordinate of an observed stop; K remains the independently observed zero-velocity event. The existing solver may evaluate force microscopically beyond the root in its terminal step, but no such state or area is exposed as forward motion. If the finite numerical run stalls (including an approach to zero force and zero K), only actually recorded forward motion is exposed; no negative K or fabricated completion.

No power, potential-energy definition, return-trip dynamics, friction law, drag, or arbitrary multi-dimensional paths are owned here. The force is a prescribed net spatial field on a fixed horizontal track, not a time history. Presets are explicitly resettable; ungraded account reveal is learner-controlled, not falsely claimed to be gated by Predict completion.
