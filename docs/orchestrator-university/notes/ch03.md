## 1. Which drag law for a thrown ball?

The lesson claims quadratic drag, not linear. Checked:

- A 90 mph (40 m/s) baseball sits at **Re ≈ 1.85 × 10⁵** — six orders of magnitude above
  the Stokes regime, deep in the range where the drag force goes as $v^2$. Quadratic drag
  "totally dominates" at 80–100 mph ball speeds.
- Measured **C_d ≈ 0.35** for a spinning pitched baseball; as low as ~0.26 for a
  non-spinning ball caught in the drag crisis. 0.3–0.4 is the working band.
- Sanity check the lesson can quote: the drag force **exceeds the weight** above roughly
  95 mph. That is the same statement as "terminal speed is a bit under 95 mph", and our
  computed terminal speed is 39.7 m/s = 89 mph. The model agrees with the literature to
  within the C_d uncertainty, which is the honest precision here.

Sources: [Nathan, *Simplified Models for the Drag Coefficient of a Pitched Baseball*
(TPT 2014)](https://baseball.physics.illinois.edu/DragTPTMay2014.pdf) ·
[baseballaero.com, *Baseball Drag Crisis*](https://baseballaero.com/2019/05/03/baseball-drag-crisis/) ·
[Illinois Phys 325, *Drag Force Intuition*](https://courses.physics.illinois.edu/phys325/fa2014/discussion/Disc3.pdf)

### Converting to the library's parameter

`launchProjectile({ drag })` uses $\vec a_\text{drag} = -k\,|v|\,\vec v$ with $k$ in 1/m, so

$$k = \frac{\tfrac12 \rho C_d A}{m}, \qquad v_\infty = \sqrt{g/k}.$$

`ballDrag()` in `motion2d.ts` does this conversion, and `BALLS` carries the specs, so no
lesson ever types a drag coefficient as a bare number.

| ball | m (kg) | r (m) | C_d | k (1/m) | v∞ (m/s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseball | 0.145 | 0.0366 | 0.35 | 6.2 × 10⁻³ | 39.7 |
| soccer ball | 0.430 | 0.110 | 0.25 | 1.35 × 10⁻² | 26.9 |
| ping-pong ball | 0.0027 | 0.020 | 0.45 | 0.128 | 8.8 |

The ping-pong ball is in the pack deliberately: it is the case where "the parabola" is
simply wrong, and it makes the drag lesson's edge beat land without needing a hurricane.

## 2. Misconceptions — where they come from

The chapter's five, and the research they trace back to:

- **Acceleration is in the direction of motion** / **projectiles run out of force at the
  top** / **horizontal velocity dies off** are all faces of *impetus physics*: Halloun &
  Hestenes describe the naive three-stage trajectory — impetus suppresses gravity and
  drives the projectile horizontally, then a middle stage compromises with gravity, then
  the object falls vertically once impetus is "exhausted". The underlying prescientific
  belief is that *every motion has a cause*, so constant velocity needs a maintaining
  force. This is why lesson 2's strobe comparison is the whole argument: the vertical
  spacing of a launched ball's strobes is identical to a dropped ball's, which kills the
  three-stage story with a picture instead of a sentence.
  [Halloun & Hestenes, *Common sense concepts about motion*, AJP 53 (1985)](https://www.halloun.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/CS-Concepts_AJP85.pdf)
- **Circular motion needs a force outward.** Documented as a persistent belief that a real
  radially-outward force acts on a body on a circular path. Lesson 1 makes it falsifiable
  rather than arguable: the learner can point the acceleration arrow outward, and the path
  spirals away instead of closing.
  [Modified module analysis of FCI misconception structure (arXiv 1905.06176)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.06176)
- **Relative velocity is a trick, not a change of frame** — not covered by these two
  lessons. Left for a chapter 3 lesson 3 with the reserved ids `relative-velocity`,
  `galilean-addition`, and `position-vector` / `angular-speed` still unclaimed.

## 3. Numbers the lessons print, and where they come from

| claim | computed by | pinned by |
|---|---|---|
| perpendicular constant-|a| ⇒ circle of radius $v^2/a$, period $2\pi v/a$ | `steerRun` + `centripetal` | `motion2d.test.ts` "holds a circle" |
| speed changes only through $a_t$ | `tangentNormalSplit` | "speed rate equals the tangential component" |
| speeding up while a points left is possible | `steerRun` | "an object can speed up while its acceleration points left" |
| $y(t)$ of a 45° launch = $y(t)$ of a vertical launch with the same $v_{y0}$ | `launchProjectile` | "independence" |
| drag-free measured range = `idealRange` | `flightMetrics` | "closed-form range" |
| with drag: range < ideal, descent longer than climb, impact slower than launch | `flightMetrics` | "drag breaks every symmetry of the parabola" |
| terminal speed $\sqrt{g/k}$ | `terminalSpeed` | "a long drop approaches the terminal speed" |

## 4. Things deliberately not done

- **No three.js.** The steering world is 2D canvas; per-pixel resolution is not the point
  here, a draggable arrow is.
- **No new integrator.** `launchProjectile` (velocity Verlet, drag-aware) and
  `tangentNormalSplit` / `centripetal` already existed in `kinematics.ts`; `motion2d.ts`
  only adds the 2D steering stepper, the flight metrics, and the ball specs.
- `angular-speed` is *reported* by the steering widget (period and ω appear once the path
  closes) but not claimed as an owned concept — lesson 3 should own it properly rather
  than have it mentioned in passing here.
