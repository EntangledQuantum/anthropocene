## Linearise, solve, repeat

At the current $x$, replace $F$ by its tangent and solve for the intercept:

$$
F(x) + J(x)\,\delta = 0 \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad J(x)\,\delta = -F(x),
$$

then $x \leftarrow x + \delta$. That linear system *is* the step. In one unknown it is a division. In $n$ unknowns it is a linear solve you already own: [conjugate gradient](/anthropocene/learn/linear-algebra/iterative/krylov/) when $J$ is SPD, another Krylov method when it is not. The outer iteration does not care how you invert $J$. It cares whether $x$ was in the basin.

Drag $x_0$ onto the cyan band. Residual: $10^{-1}$, $10^{-3}$, $10^{-6}$, $10^{-13}$. That accelerating drop is quadratic. Drag it onto magenta. Same formula, same $F$, and the iterates fly.

The cyan band is the basin of attraction. Quadratic is what happens *once you are in it*.

## Did the linear solve fail, or did Newton walk off a cliff?

A small residual of $J\delta + F$ means the step was the Newton step. It does not mean $x$ was in the basin. [Conditioning of $J$](/anthropocene/learn/computational-physics/numerical-reality/conditioning-vs-stability/) is a third question: tiny $J\delta + F$ and a wrong $x$ when $\kappa(J)$ ate the digits.

<Recall id="nt-basin-card" concept="newtons-method">
  <div slot="front">What does Newton actually do at each step, and what decides whether that step helps?</div>

It linearises: solve $J(x)\,\delta = -F(x)$, then $x \leftarrow x + \delta$. Quadratic catch once $x$ is in the basin of attraction. Outside it the same operator diverges. A wrong $J$ (Picard, frozen, a sloppy inner solve) is a different iteration with a different basin and a different rate.

</Recall>

<Tier level="advanced">

Inexact Newton accepts an approximate $\delta$ as long as $\|J\delta + F\| \le \eta\|F\|$. Keep $\eta$ bounded away from 1 and you get linear; send $\eta \to 0$ and you get superlinear; $\eta = O(\|F\|)$ recovers quadratic. Jacobian-free Newton–Krylov never forms $J$: it probes $Jv \approx \bigl(F(x+\varepsilon v)-F(x)\bigr)/\varepsilon$ and feeds that matvec to Krylov. You already own the inner method.

When the basin is a speck you do not take a "better Newton step". You continue: embed $F$ in a homotopy or a pseudo-transient, walk a curve of solvable problems into the basin, then Newton finishes.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

The picture is the basin. Quadratic once you are in it. A bad Jacobian is a different operator. If it exploded, ask which of those three you are looking at before you blame the linear solver.
