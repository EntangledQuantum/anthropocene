## $M$ is a cheap fake inverse

You never form $A^{-1}$. You also do not have to feed Krylov $A$. You feed it
$M^{-1}A$, where $M$ is SPD, cheap to apply, and *close to $A$ as an operator* —
close enough that the eigenvalues of $M^{-1}A$ sit in a clump instead of
spreading from $\lambda_{\min}$ to $\lambda_{\max}$.

$$
\kappa(M^{-1}A) = \frac{\lambda_{\max}(M^{-1}A)}{\lambda_{\min}(M^{-1}A)} \ll \kappa(A)
$$

is the hope. Clustering is the mechanism: a matrix with $m$ tight clumps is
a polynomial of degree $m$ away from done, even if $n$ is large.

The extra line in the recurrence is one apply of $M^{-1}$:

```ts
const Ap = applyA(p);                    // the only matvec
const z = applyMinv(r);                  // the cheap fake inverse
const alpha = dot(r, z) / dot(p, Ap);
x = axpy(alpha, p, x);
r = axpy(-alpha, Ap, r);
p = axpy(dot(r, z) / rzOld, p, z);      // search along z, not r
```

Same short recurrence. Residuals are now $M^{-1}$-orthogonal. Search
directions stay $A$-orthogonal. Krylov did not change. The operator did.

</Predict>

## SSOR is two sweeps you already know

Symmetric Gauss–Seidel, over-relaxed. One forward bidiagonal solve, a
diagonal scale, one backward. On this $A$,

$$
M = \frac{1}{\omega(2-\omega)}\,(D+\omega L)\,D^{-1}\,(D+\omega U).
$$

Switch the lab to **SSOR**. The magenta eigenvalues pile up. κ drops from
116 to about 6. The residual is not waiting for $k = n$.

<Recall id="pc-recall-precond" concept="preconditioning">
  <div slot="front">What is a preconditioner, and why is Krylov not the algorithm?</div>

A preconditioner is a cheap fake inverse $M \approx A$. You iterate on $M^{-1}A$
so the eigenvalues bunch and $\kappa$ drops. CG is the same short recurrence
it always was — the accelerator. On this Laplacian, Jacobi $M = D$ is a scale
and does nothing; SSOR is two bidiagonal solves and $\kappa$ falls from 116 to 6.

</Recall>

<Tier level="advanced" title="left / right / split, ILU on a tridiagonal, and when Jacobi wakes up">

Left-preconditioning writes $M^{-1}Ax = M^{-1}b$. Right writes $AM^{-1}y = b$,
$x = M^{-1}y$. Split writes $L^{-1} A L^{-T}$ when $M = LL^{T}$. For CG the
PCG recurrence above *is* the split form: it keeps the $A$-inner product and
never forms $M^{-1}A$. On a nonsymmetric $A$ the three choices stop being
equivalent, and that is GMRES's problem, later.

ILU(0) on this tridiagonal $A$ is exact LU. $M = A$, every eigenvalue of
$M^{-1}A$ is 1, and you have a direct solve wearing a preconditioner badge.
The 1D costume is too kind. In 2D the fill is the point, and ILU is
approximate on purpose.

Jacobi starts helping the moment $D$ is not a scale — a rod with two
conductivities, a stretched mesh, anything whose diagonal actually varies.
Then $D^{-1}A$ really is a different operator. This constant-coefficient
Poisson is the case that hides it, which is why it is the right first
picture: the cheapest $M$ can be a no-op, and you have to look at the
spectrum to know.

Multigrid as $M$ is the next cheap fake inverse, and it is also a solver.
That is the following lesson.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

Krylov is an accelerator. The algorithm is $M^{-1}A$. On this Poisson the
diagonal is a scale and does nothing; SSOR bunches the eigenvalues and
CG finishes in half the steps. Next, the leftover long wave on a coarser
grid — a fake inverse built from two meshes.
