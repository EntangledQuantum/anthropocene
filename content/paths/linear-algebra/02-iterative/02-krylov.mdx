## The span of what $A$ can tell you

Start from $r_0 = b - Ax_0$. Apply $A$. You now own two vectors. Apply it
again. The only subspace those matvecs have earned is

$$
K_k = \operatorname{span}\{r_0,\, Ar_0,\, A^2 r_0,\, \ldots,\, A^{k-1} r_0\}.
$$

That is the Krylov plane. There is no second object behind it — the same
loop that *is* $A$ in [the operator lesson](/anthropocene/learn/linear-algebra/operators/matrix-as-operator/).

CG takes the unique $x_k \in x_0 + K_k$ that minimises $\|x^* - x\|_A$. Equivalently: the residual $r_k$ is orthogonal to the whole plane $K_k$. That is the $A$-best residual you can pick without another matvec.

</Predict>

## Not gradient descent

Steepest descent also line-searches. Its next residual is orthogonal to the
*last* residual. Then it steps along the new residual, which is not
$A$-orthogonal to the last search, and it undoes work.

CG keeps the residuals orthogonal to **every** previous residual, by making
the search directions $A$-orthogonal — conjugate diameters of the energy
ellipse.

```ts
const Ap = applyA(p);                    // the only matvec
const alpha = dot(r, r) / dot(p, Ap);
x = axpy(alpha, p, x);
r = axpy(-alpha, Ap, r);
p = axpy(dot(r, r) / rrOld, p, r);      // A-orthogonalize against the last p
```

One matvec. A three-term recurrence. The next residual stands perpendicular
to the last one.

<Recall id="cg-recall-cg" concept="conjugate-gradient">
  <div slot="front">What does CG minimise at step k, over what set, and why is that not steepest descent?</div>

CG minimises $\|x^* - x\|_A$ over $x \in x_0 + K_k$. Steepest descent
minimises energy only along the current residual, then retraces. CG's
search directions are $A$-orthogonal, so the residual is orthogonal to the
whole plane so far and no previous step is undone. On SPD $A$, that finishes
in $n$ steps.

</Recall>

<Tier level="advanced" title="the rate, clustering, and where this stops">

The $A$-norm error after $k$ steps satisfies

$$
\frac{\|e_k\|_A}{\|e_0\|_A} \le 2 \left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}\right)^k,
$$

with $\kappa = \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$. For this Dirichlet Laplacian
$\kappa \sim n^2$, so the bound is $O(n)$ iterations — and the overlay you
watched is that bound being pessimistic, because CG cares about the *whole*
spectrum. Clustered eigenvalues shrink the effective Krylov dimension: a
matrix with $m$ distinct eigenvalues is done in $m$ steps.

The Euclidean residual is not monotone. $\|e\|_A$ is. Looking at $\|r\|_2$
go up on the first step is not instability; it is CG refusing to minimise
the wrong norm.

This lesson stops before the next two facts, on purpose. If $A$ is not SPD,
the $A$-norm is not a norm and the short recurrence breaks — that is MINRES
and GMRES, later. A cheap fake inverse that clusters the spectrum is a
preconditioner, and it is the algorithm; Krylov is then the accelerator.
Neither is in this plane.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

$K_k = \operatorname{span}\{r, Ar, \ldots\}$ is everything matvecs can tell
you from one residual. CG picks the $A$-best point in that plane: residuals
orthogonal, search directions $A$-orthogonal, exact in $n$ steps. Jacobi
will not catch it. The picture to keep is the next residual standing
perpendicular to the last one.
