## Same plane, different inner product

$K_k = \operatorname{span}\{r, Ar, \ldots, A^{k-1}r\}$ is still everything $k$ matvecs can tell you. [CG](/anthropocene/learn/linear-algebra/iterative/krylov/) picked the $A$-best point in $x_0 + K_k$. That $A$-norm needs $A$ SPD.

GMRES keeps the plane and changes the question:

$$
x_k = \arg\min_{x \in x_0 + K_k} \|b - Ax\|_2.
$$

Arnoldi builds an orthonormal basis $V_k$ of that plane and a $(k+1)\times k$ upper Hessenberg $\bar H$ with $A V_k = V_{k+1}\bar H$. The least-squares problem on $\bar H$ is [Householder QR](/anthropocene/learn/linear-algebra/factorizations/qr/) of a postcard. The last entry of the rotated right-hand side *is* $\|r\|_k$. You know the residual before you form $x$.

```ts
let w = applyA(V[j]);
for (const vi of V) w = axpy(-dot(w, vi), vi, w);  // modified Gram–Schmidt
V.push(scale(w, 1 / norm2(w)));
// QR of H̄: min ‖β e₁ − H̄ y‖, then x = x₀ + V y
```

One new vector per step. Every previous vector stays.

## The cost is the basis

CG kept two vectors and a three-term recurrence. That was the gift of conjugacy. GMRES has no such gift: each new Arnoldi vector is orthogonalised against **every** previous one, and they all stay in RAM.

Restart every $m$ steps: discard $V$, take the current $x$ as the new $x_0$, build a fresh plane from the leftover residual. You cap RAM at $m$ vectors. You also cap the search: the minimiser over $K_k$ is gone.

<Recall id="gm-recall-gmres" concept="gmres">
  <div slot="front">What does GMRES minimise at step k, over what set, and what is the cost?</div>

GMRES minimises $\|b-Ax\|_2$ over $x \in x_0 + K_k$. Arnoldi builds an orthonormal basis of that plane; the least-squares problem is QR of a tiny Hessenberg. The cost is storing every basis vector. Restart every $m$ steps caps RAM at $m$ and gives up the global minimiser.

</Recall>

<Tier level="advanced" title="Givens without x, positive-real vs SPD, and a stall">

The Givens QR of $\bar H$ rotates the right-hand side $\beta e_1$ into $g$. Then $\|r_k\|_2 = |g_{k+1}|$ — the residual of a problem you have not formed $x$ for. That is why a GMRES log can be honest at every step without an extra matvec.

Convection–diffusion is *positive real*: $x^\top A x = x^\top(-\nabla^2)x > 0$, so CG's $p^\top A p$ stays positive and the loop does not break. It just is not CG. A $90^\circ$ rotation $A = R(\pi/2)\,\mathrm{diag}(2,1/2)$ is worse: $p^\top A p$ is a rounding hair, and two CG steps send $\|r\|$ to $10^{16}$. GMRES is exact at $k = 2$.

If the residual stalls with $m$ already large, the next question is not "more iterations." It is: is $A$ bad, is the preconditioner bad, or is the physics singular? GMRES will not invent a solution that is not in the range.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

$K_k$ is still the plane. GMRES picks the Euclidean-best residual in it, so $A$ need not be SPD. You store the whole basis. Restart trades that optimality for RAM. The picture to keep is the Hessenberg growing by one column per step — and collapsing when you cannot afford it.
