## A local average is a low-pass filter

Jacobi writes, at every unknown,

$$
u_i \leftarrow (1-\omega)\,u_i + \omega\,\frac{u_{i-1}+u_{i+1}+h^2 f_i}{2}.
$$

The Dirichlet sine modes of $A$ are eigenvectors of that map. Mode $k$ is multiplied by

$$
\mu_k = 1 - 2\omega\sin^2\!\bigl(k\pi / (2(n+1))\bigr).
$$

For $\omega = 2/3$ and $k \ge n/2$, $|\mu_k| \le 1/3$. For $k = 1$, $\mu_1 \approx 1 - O(h^2)$. High-k dies. Low-k lives. That split *is* smoothing.

## As a solver it is the long wave, slowly

The spectral radius of undamped Jacobi on this $A$ is the worst $|\mu_k|$,

$$
\rho = \cos\bigl(\pi/(n+1)\bigr) \approx 1 - \tfrac{\pi^2}{2}h^2.
$$

On $n = 31$ that is $0.995$. Each sweep of the leftover multiplies by $0.995$.

## Gauss–Seidel is the same average, using what you just wrote

Walk left to right. At node $i$ the left neighbour is already new:

$$
u_i \leftarrow \frac{u_{i-1}^{\mathrm{new}} + u_{i+1}^{\mathrm{old}} + h^2 f_i}{2}.
$$

$$
u_i \leftarrow (1-\omega)\,u_i + \omega\,\frac{u_{i-1}+u_{i+1}+h^2 f_i}{2}.
$$

On this Laplacian the sine modes are eigenvectors with $\mu_k = 1 - 2\omega\sin^2(k\pi / (2(n+1)))$. The spectral radius is $\mu_1 = \cos(\pi/(n+1))$ when $\omega = 1$, which sits at $1 - O(h^2)$. That is the long wave. Jacobi (and GS) are smoothers.

</Recall>

<Tier level="advanced" title="n = 3, the Nyquist mode, and why the stall is a coarse grid">

On $n = 3$ the Jacobi factors are $\cos(\pi/4)$, $0$, $-\cos(\pi/4)$. The middle mode dies in one sweep. The two others stall at $1/\sqrt{2}$. The whole lesson, three unknowns.

Undamped Jacobi ($\omega = 1$) has $|\mu_n| = \cos(\pi/(n+1))$ too — the Nyquist mode stalls with the long wave. That is why the smoother is weighted. $\omega = 2/3$ puts $|\mu| \le 1/3$ on the whole high-k half of the spectrum, and makes $\rho$ *worse* (closer to 1). A better smoother is a worse solver. Those are different jobs.

Gauss–Seidel on this tridiagonal $A$ has eigenvalues $\cos^2(k\pi/(n+1))$, so $\rho_{\mathrm{GS}} = \rho_J^2$. The sine modes are *not* GS eigenvectors — the iteration matrix is not symmetric — but the leftover is still a long wave. Red-black GS is a later costume of the same split.

As a solver, iterations to cut the error by a constant factor scale like $1/(1-\rho) \sim n^2$. Refine, wait longer. As a smoother, five sweeps suffice at every $h$. The long-wave leftover is a well-resolved sine on a grid twice as coarse. That is the next move, and it is not this lesson.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

High-k dies, low-k lives. After a few sweeps the residual is smooth and then it stops. That stall is not a failure of Jacobi — it is the definition of a smoother, and it is the error a coarse grid can see. Next, a different subspace: what matvecs can tell you from one residual.
