A unit circle. This matrix:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

The unit *square* becomes a parallelogram with sides $(1,0)$ and $(1,1)$ — the columns. What does the unit *circle* become?

## Two stretches and the rotations that aim them

$A$ does three things, in order:

$$
A = U\,\Sigma\,V^{\mathsf T}.
$$

$V^{\mathsf T}$ rotates the circle so its axes sit on $e_1, e_2$. $\Sigma = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)$ stretches those axes — that is the two stretches of the title. $U$ rotates the ellipse into place. $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge 0$ are the axis lengths.

For this shear they are the golden ratio and its reciprocal: $\sigma_1 = \varphi \approx 1.618$, $\sigma_2 = 1/\varphi \approx 0.618$.

[Householder QR](/anthropocene/learn/linear-algebra/factorizations/qr/) wrote one orthogonal factor. SVD writes two, and isolates the stretch on the diagonal. Orthogonal $U$ and $V$ do not stretch, so the whole stretch of $A$ is $\Sigma$.

Drag $\sigma_2$. The axes stay put. The ellipse flattens. Set rank $k = 1$ and the ellipse collapses to the major-axis segment — the best rank-1 picture of $A$.

## The stretch the eigenvalues hide

Both eigenvalues of this shear are $1$. A Jordan block; not even diagonalizable. $|\lambda_{\max}|/|\lambda_{\min}| = 1$.

## Drop the small stretch

The rank-$k$ picture is the SVD with $\sigma_{k+1},\sigma_{k+2},\ldots$ set to zero:

$$
A_k = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i\, u_i v_i^{\mathsf T}.
$$

Eckart–Young: among all matrices of rank at most $k$, this $A_k$ is the closest to $A$ in the 2-norm, and the leftover *is* the first dropped $\sigma$:

$$
\|A - A_k\|_2 = \sigma_{k+1}.
$$

<Recall id="svd-decomposition" concept="svd">
  <div slot="front">What does A = U Σ Vᵀ do to the unit circle, and where is κ₂ in that picture?</div>

$V^{\mathsf T}$ rotates, $\Sigma$ stretches the axes by $\sigma_1 \ge \cdots \ge \sigma_n \ge 0$, $U$ rotates the ellipse into place. The axis lengths *are* the singular values. $\kappa_2(A) = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}$. Eigenvalues of a non-normal $A$ can hide that stretch completely.

</Recall>

<Recall id="svd-eckart-young" concept="low-rank">
  <div slot="front">What is the closest rank-k matrix to A in 2-norm, and what leftover do you pay?</div>

The truncated SVD $A_k = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\mathsf T}$. Eckart–Young: $\|A - A_k\|_2 = \sigma_{k+1}$. Zeroing small entries of $A$ is a different, worse, not-even-rank-$k$ move.

</Recall>

<Tier level="advanced" title="polar form, and why this SVD is not the one you ship">

$A = U_p P$ with $P = \sqrt{A^{\mathsf T}A}$ SPD (the stretch) and $U_p$ orthogonal (the rotation) is the polar decomposition. SVD factors $P$ further: $P = V\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}$, and $U_p = UV^{\mathsf T}$. One stretch, then a split onto coordinate axes.

$V$ *is* the eigenvector matrix of $A^{\mathsf T}A$, and $U$ of $AA^{\mathsf T}$. Forming $A^{\mathsf T}A$ to compute them squares $\kappa$, the sin the [QR lesson](/anthropocene/learn/linear-algebra/factorizations/qr/) exists to name. The 2×2 and 8×8 here can afford it. A production SVD bidiagonalises with Householder (Golub–Kahan) and never writes the Gram.

Thin SVD: $U$ is $m\times r$, $\Sigma$ is $r\times r$, $V$ is $n\times r$, $r = \mathrm{rank}(A)$. Full SVD pads with bases for $\ker A$ and $\ker A^{\mathsf T}$. Compression uses the thin one, truncated.

Mirsky: the same $A_k$ also minimises every unitarily invariant norm, including Frobenius, where the leftover is $\sqrt{\sigma_{k+1}^2 + \cdots + \sigma_n^2}$.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

$A = U\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}$. The unit circle becomes an ellipse; $\sigma$ are the axes; $\kappa_2 = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}$.

Drop small $\sigma$. Eckart–Young: that is the best rank-$k$ picture, and the leftover 2-norm is the first one you dropped.
