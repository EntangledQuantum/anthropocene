Overlap of two discs, then the linear spring–dashpot of Cundall & Strack (1979):

$$
\delta = R_i + R_j - |x_i - x_j|, \qquad
F_n = k_n \delta - \gamma_n v_n \quad (\delta > 0)
$$

and $F_n = 0$ otherwise. $v_n$ is the relative speed along the line of centres. Tangential: a second spring, truncated at $\mu F_n$. That is the whole material.

The dashpot is why a bounce is not elastic. Reduced mass $m^* = m/2$ for equal discs; damping ratio $\zeta = \gamma_n / (2\sqrt{k_n m^*})$. Underdamped, the outgoing relative speed is

$$
e = \exp\bigl(-\zeta\pi / \sqrt{1-\zeta^2}\bigr).
$$

A grain sitting on a floor is the same spring, at rest: $k_n \delta = mg$. Take $k_n \to \infty$ and the overlap vanishes. That is the hard-sphere limit, and it kills the explicit step, because the contact duration is $t_c \sim \pi\sqrt{m^*/k_n}$.

<Recall id="dem-one-line" concept="discrete-element">
  <div slot="front">Where does a sandpile’s constitutive law live?</div>

At the contacts. $F_n = k_n\delta - \gamma_n v_n$ for $\delta > 0$, and zero otherwise. The bulk has no $\sigma(\varepsilon)$. Angle of repose, hopper discharge, a bounce — the same local law, counted out over a network. Overlap is a penalty for rigidity: $k\to\infty$ recovers the hard constraint and kills the explicit step.

</Recall>

<Tier level="advanced" title="Hertz, rolling resistance, and the penalty">

Hertz contact is $F_n \propto \delta^{3/2}$ for elastic spheres. The linear spring is the pedagogical cousin: same hockey-stick, constant $t_c$, an $e$ you can write in closed form. Production DEM often uses Hertz–Mindlin plus a history spring; the picture does not change.

Perfect discs with sliding friction still roll. A single disc on an incline rolls at any angle. Rolling resistance (Ai, Chen, Rotter, Ooi 2011) is a Coulomb torque that stands in for shape. Turn it off and a 2D pile slumps even with $\mu > 0$. That is why this lab’s $\mu$ slider also scales $\mu_r$.

The explicit step has to resolve $t_c$. A common rule is $\Delta t \approx 0.1\, t_c$ with $t_c = \pi / \sqrt{k_n/m^* - (\gamma_n/2m^*)^2}$. Stiffer contacts, smaller overlap, more steps. Coarse-graining into a continuum stress (the inverse of this lesson) is how you leave that trap. Coupling DEM–FEM or DEM–MPM puts the penalty on one side of a wall and a field on the other.

True rigidity is the [constrained](../../structure-preserving/constraints/) map you already have: a multiplier, not a spring. Non-smooth contact (Moreau–Jean) is that costume for grains. Soft-sphere DEM is what you run when you can afford the penalty.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

Overlap → force. The pile’s slope is the contact law, once.

MD is a potential at every $r$. A constraint lands on $\delta = 0$. DEM is the penalty in between, and $\Delta t$ is the price.
