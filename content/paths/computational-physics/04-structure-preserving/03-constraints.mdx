A pendulum is a particle that lives on a circle. Write it in Cartesian coordinates
and the law is $g(q)=\tfrac12(x^2+y^2-1)=0$. Differentiate twice, solve for the
multiplier $\lambda$, and you have an ordinary ODE on $\mathbb{R}^4$ whose *flow*
stays on that circle. Hand the ODE to RK4.

The constraint of the continuous system is not a constraint of the discrete map
unless the map is built to land on it. That is the whole idea, in one sentence.

## The tempting cleanup

The obvious fix: after every step, set $q \leftarrow q/|q|$ and strip the radial
velocity. The particle sits on the circle again. Job done?

SHAKE (Ryckaert, Ciccotti, Berendsen 1977) is [velocity Verlet](../verlet-vs-rk4/)
with one extra unknown. For this spherical constraint:

```ts
const qUnc = q + h * v + 0.5 * h * h * f;   // f = (0, −1), gravity only
const alpha = shakeAlpha(q, qUnc);           // Newton: |qUnc − α q|² = 1
const qNext = qUnc - alpha * q;             // force along ∇g(q_n)
```

$\alpha = \tfrac12 h^2\lambda$ is chosen so $g(q_{n+1})=0$. The particle never
leaves. RATTLE (Andersen 1983) then picks a second multiplier so
$q_{n+1}\cdot v_{n+1}=0$ — and for a sphere, that second correction is exactly
the tangent projection of the Verlet-completed velocity.

Switch the lab to **SHAKE**, then **RATTLE**, then **RK4 + project**. SHAKE
pins $g$ and leaves $q\cdot v$ at $O(h)$. RATTLE pins both, to roundoff.
Projection pins both too — and the particle is not where RATTLE put it.

<Recall id="cons-map-not-cleanup" concept="constrained-integration">
  <div slot="front">Why is dividing by |q| after a step not the same as SHAKE?</div>

SHAKE chooses the Lagrange multiplier so the Verlet step *itself* lands on
$g(q_{n+1})=0$, with the constraint force along $\nabla g$ at the old position.
Dividing by $|q|$ is a different map: it pulls along the new radius, after an
update that was never asked to know about $g$. Same circle. Different discrete
mechanics.

</Recall>

## A rotation that is not a rotation

Same structure, different clothing. A rigid body lives on $SO(3)$:
$R^\top R = I$, $\det R = +1$. The kinematics are $\dot R = R\hat\omega$.
RK4 on the nine entries is the index-reduced move: the *flow* stays in the
group, the discrete map does not.

The cube is $Rv$ for the eight vertices of a cube. When $R$ leaves $SO(3)$ the
cube shears. Switch to **Lie-group exp**: $R_{n+1}=R_n\exp(h\hat\omega)$
(Rodrigues). The cube stays a cube because the discrete map was defined on the
group.

<Recall id="cons-shake-rattle" concept="constrained-integration">
  <div slot="front">What does SHAKE impose, what does RATTLE add, and why is a Lie-group step different from both?</div>

SHAKE: $g(q_{n+1})=0$ as part of a Verlet step, force along $\nabla g(q_n)$.
RATTLE: also $q_{n+1}\cdot v_{n+1}=0$, so the state stays on the tangent bundle.

A Lie-group step never leaves: $R_{n+1}=R_n\exp(h\hat\omega)$ is in $SO(3)$
because the exponential of $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ *is* $SO(3)$. There is no residual
to project. The constraint is the domain of the map.

</Recall>

<Tier level="advanced" title="why the cleanup is the wrong map">

SHAKE and RATTLE are symplectic (Leimkuhler & Skeel 1994). They are the
constrained analogue of the [discrete action](../variational-view/) that gave
you Verlet: the multiplier is a discrete Euler–Lagrange unknown, not a
post-process.

Naive projection after an unconstrained or index-reduced step generally
destroys that. It can sit on $TM$ and still fail to be a symplectic map, still
fail to have a shadow Hamiltonian, still fail to be the variational update.
The tests in `src/lib/numerics/constraints.ts` pin the cheap version of this:
after forty steps the projected RK4 state is a visible distance from RATTLE,
while both report $g=0$.

For a sphere the RATTLE velocity correction *looks* like a projection. The
difference is what it is projecting: the Verlet-completed velocity of a step
whose $\lambda$ was chosen to hit $g(q_{n+1})=0$ along $\nabla g(q_n)$. Projecting
RK4’s velocity is projecting a different vector.

</Tier>

<Tier level="frontier" title="index, hidden constraints, Lie algebra">

Holonomic $g(q)=0$ is an index-3 DAE. Differentiating twice drops the index
and produces an ODE whose invariant manifold is unstable under discretisation
— the drift you just drew. Baumgarte stabilisation adds a feedback law to
that ODE; it is a different compromise, not a structure-preserving map.

The hidden constraint $\dot g = G(q)v=0$ is why SHAKE and RATTLE disagree at
integer times. Viewed at half-steps they generate the same $(q,p_{n+1/2})$
sequence; RATTLE is the one-step map on $TM$.

On a Lie group the algebra is the tangent space at the identity. Munthe–Kaas
lifts an RK method to the algebra and retracts with $\exp$ (or Cayley, or
canonical coordinates of the second kind). The order-1 case is the step you
ran. Higher order is the same idea with commutators. The point does not
change: if the retraction lands in the group, there is no residual.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

- A constraint of the flow is not a constraint of the map. If the update is
  not required to land on the manifold, the next state is a different object.
- SHAKE/RATTLE put $g(q_{n+1})=0$ *inside* the step. Lie-group methods never
  leave. Renormalising afterwards is a third map.
- Ask what set the state must live in, and whether the discrete map knows.
