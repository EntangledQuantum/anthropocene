The last lesson treated velocity Verlet as a reordering of Euler, then explained its bounded energy with a [shadow Hamiltonian](../verlet-vs-rk4/). That is one origin. Here is another, and it is the cleaner one.

You do not discretise Newton's equation. You discretise the **action**. Stationarity of that discrete action *is* the update — and it is Verlet. Conservation laws then fall out of symmetries, the same way they do in the continuous theory. One of them does not survive.

Area holding is not a lucky property of this vector field. Verlet was never asked to preserve area. It is what you get when a discrete action is stationary.

## A broken path still has an action

The continuous story: a path $q(t)$ between two times has action $S = \int L(q, \dot q)\,dt$, and the true motion is a stationary point of $S$. For $L = \tfrac12 |\dot q|^2 - V(q)$ that stationarity is Newton.

Now replace the smooth path by a **broken** one — positions $q_0, q_1, \ldots, q_N$ at a fixed step $h$, straight segments in between. The action becomes a sum of discrete Lagrangians. The natural midpoint discretisation uses the velocity at the centre of each interval and averages the potential at the two endpoints:

$$
L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}) = h\left[\frac12\left\|\frac{q_{k+1}-q_k}{h}\right\|^2 - \frac{V(q_k)+V(q_{k+1})}{2}\right]
$$

That is the action of one segment. The discrete action is $S_d = \sum_k L_d(q_k, q_{k+1})$. Vary the interior points and demand $\delta S_d = 0$. What remains is the discrete Euler–Lagrange equation

$$
D_2 L_d(q_{k-1}, q_k) + D_1 L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}) = 0.
$$

For this $L_d$ it expands to one line:

$$
\frac{q_{k+1} - 2q_k + q_{k-1}}{h^2} = -\nabla V(q_k).
$$

That is position Verlet. The discrete Legendre transform $p_k = -D_1 L_d(q_k, q_{k+1})$ turns it into the velocity form you already ran:

```ts
const a  = accel(q);
const qNext = q + h * v + 0.5 * h * h * a;     // from p = −D1 Ld
const aNext = accel(qNext);
const vNext = v + 0.5 * h * (a + aNext);       // from p₊ = D2 Ld
```

Same code as `velocityVerlet` in `src/lib/numerics/ode.ts`. The library does not grow a second stepper. The discrete action *is* that stepper.

## Which symmetries survive the break

Noether's theorem does not care that the path is broken. If a one-parameter family of transformations leaves $L_d$ invariant, the corresponding discrete momentum map is **constant along the discrete trajectory** — to machine precision, not to $\Order(h^p)$.

That is the discrete Noether theorem (Marsden & West, theorem 1.3.3). It is stricter than "the method is accurate, so conserved quantities are approximately conserved."

The catch is which symmetries $L_d$ still has.

- **Rotation.** $V$ depends only on $|q|$ (Kepler, any central force). Rotating $q_k$ and $q_{k+1}$ together leaves $L_d$ unchanged, so angular momentum $q \times p$ is exact.
- **Translation.** $V$ depends only on differences $q_a - q_b$ (an isolated $N$-body). Shifting every particle together leaves $L_d$ unchanged, so total linear momentum is exact.
- **Time translation.** The continuous Lagrangian does not depend on $t$, so energy is conserved. The discrete Lagrangian lives on pairs $(q_k, q_{k+1})$ at a **fixed** $h$. There is no continuous time left to shift. Energy is not a discrete Noether quantity. It stays within $\Order(h^2)$ of a nearby [shadow Hamiltonian](../verlet-vs-rk4/) forever — bounded, not exact.

## The movie plays backwards

The same $L_d$ is unchanged if you swap the two arguments: $L_d(q_0, q_1) = L_d(q_1, q_0)$. The action of a broken path equals the action of its reverse. Stationary paths therefore run equally well backwards, which is the statement

$$
\Phi_h^{-1} = \Phi_{-h}.
$$

Equivalently: reverse all velocities, step, reverse again, and you are back where you started — to machine precision, for any $h$ the map is defined.

That is time-reversibility. It is not a certificate of accuracy. It is a symmetry of the discrete action. A method that dissipates energy cannot equal its own reverse, which is why reversible maps cannot show the monotone drift RK4 does.

<Recall id="var-reverse-card" concept="time-reversibility">
  <div slot="front">What does time-reversibility of the map say, and why does a discrete action give it for free?</div>

Stepping forward by $h$ and then backward by $h$ is the identity: the discrete map is its own reverse.

$L_d(q_0, q_1) = L_d(q_1, q_0)$ — the action of a segment equals the action of the reversed segment — so a stationary broken path remains stationary when you run it backwards. Verlet inherits this; classical RK4 does not (its Butcher tableau is not palindromic), and neither does forward Euler.

Reversibility forbids monotone energy drift. It does not make energy exact, and it does not make the trajectory accurate.

</Recall>

<Tier level="frontier" title="where variational goes further than symplectic-by-construction">

Imposing $\det \Phi' = 1$ by hand works for separable Hamiltonians on $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$. The discrete action still makes sense when that picture breaks.

- **Constraints.** SHAKE and RATTLE are variational integrators on $g(q) = 0$: you restrict the discrete path, not the vector field. Lagrange multipliers fall out of the same stationarity condition.
- **Collisions and contact.** A non-smooth trajectory still has an action. Variational collision integrators conserve discrete momentum through impacts because the discrete Noether argument never needed $\ddot q$ to exist.
- **Lie groups.** A rotation should stay a rotation. Discretise the action on $SO(3)$ and the update lands on the group rather than drifting off it and needing a projection.

The through-line is the same: write the principle on the space the physics actually lives on, then let stationarity produce the map.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

- Discretise the variational principle, not the ODE. The integrator is whatever makes the discrete action stationary — and for the midpoint-velocity $L_d$, that integrator is velocity Verlet.
- Spatial symmetries of $L_d$ give momenta to machine precision (discrete Noether). Time-translation dies with the choice of $h$, so energy is bounded by a shadow Hamiltonian, not exact.
- Time-reversibility is the same discrete action read backwards: $\Phi_h^{-1} = \Phi_{-h}$. A dissipative map cannot be its own reverse.
