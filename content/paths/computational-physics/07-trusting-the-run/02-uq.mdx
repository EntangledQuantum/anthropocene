That push is [Monte Carlo](/anthropocene/learn/computational-physics/other-paradigms/monte-carlo/) of the *parameter*, not of the PDE. The model is one line, so each dart is free:

```ts
function push(lambdas, T) {
  const q = [];
  for (const lam of lambdas) q.push(Math.exp(-lam * T));
  return q;
}
```

A linear map $q = a\lambda + b$ would scale the cloud by $a$ and leave its shape. Mean and variance go with $a$ and $a^2$. This map is $\exp(-\lambda T)$. Uniform $\lambda$ becomes density $1/q$ on the image of the interval — mass piles where the exponential is flat, at the small-$q$ end.

Draw that density at $T = 1$, before you see it. It starts at the slowest $\lambda$.

And the mean of that smear is not the run at mean $\lambda$. $\lambda \sim \mathrm{Uniform}[\mu - \delta, \mu + \delta]$ gives

$$
\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda T}] = e^{-\mu T}\,\frac{\sinh(\delta T)}{\delta T} \ge e^{-\mu T}
$$

with equality only at $T = 0$. Slow-decayers contribute exponentially more remaining than fast-decayers subtract. Hunt the time when the mean of the cloud sits 50% above the number you would have printed.

## A cross, then the square

Amplitude $A_0$ is also a lab number, independent, same uniform $[0.5, 1.5]$. The model is now a product:

$$
q(A_0, \lambda) = A_0 e^{-\lambda T}
$$

One-at-a-time: vary $A_0$ at mean $\lambda$, then $\lambda$ at mean $A_0$. That is a plus sign in the $(A_0, \lambda)$ square. The joint sample fills the square. The smallest remaining lives at a corner the cross never visits — smallest $A_0$ and largest $\lambda$, together.

<Recall id="uq-cloud-card" concept="uncertainty-quantification">
  <div slot="front">A model with an uncertain parameter. What is the prediction? How does it sit relative to the run at the mean?</div>

The prediction is the pushforward of the parameter distribution — a histogram, not a number. A run at the mean is one sample of that smear.

If the map bends, the sample is not even the centre: $\mathbb{E}[q(\theta)] \ne q(\mathbb{E}\theta)$. For remaining amplitude with uniform $\lambda$, the gap is $\sinh(\delta T)/(\delta T)$.

</Recall>

<Tier level="advanced" title="which input owns the bar, and what this lesson left out">

The curriculum question sitting behind the two-parameter square: **which uncertain input actually owns the error bar on the QoI?** Variance-based (Sobol) indices split $\mathrm{Var}(q)$ into main effects and interactions. For $q = A_0 e^{-\lambda T}$ the interaction term is the corner you just watched — it is not small at large $T$. OAT estimates main effects at one point and sets every interaction to zero, which is why Saltelli calls most published sensitivity analyses false.

This lesson was forward UQ by sampling. Left on the table, each a later lesson:

- **Inverse UQ / calibration.** The data are a cloud too. Bayes updates $\theta$, then you push the *posterior*. Different question.
- **Surrogates.** Polynomial chaos, collocation, a Gaussian process — so the push is cheap when $q$ is a PDE, not a one-liner. The distribution is still the prediction.
- **Model-form.** A cloud on $\lambda$ inside heat does not capture "it was advection." That one does not average out.

Numerical uncertainty is a residual-vs-$h$ slope. Parametric uncertainty is a histogram. Do not print one as the other.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

- The number came with a cloud. Push the cloud; the output smear *is* the prediction.
- Linear maps scale a cloud. Nonlinear maps fatten a tail and move the mean: $\mathbb{E}[q(\theta)] \ne q(\mathbb{E}\theta)$.
- OAT walks a cross. The extremes live at the corners.
