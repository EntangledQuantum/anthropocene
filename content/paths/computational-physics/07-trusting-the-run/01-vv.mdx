## Ask the operator a question it can fail

Pick a field that is **not** a heat eigenmode. Plug it into the PDE. Whatever is left is a source. The field is then an exact solution of the forced equation, by construction. That is the **method of manufactured solutions** (Roache). It does not have to be physics — verification is a mathematical exercise.

$$
\hat u(x,t) = \sin(2\pi x)\cos(2\pi t), \qquad
f = \hat u_t - \alpha \hat u_{xx}
$$

Run the same FTCS heat step, plus $\Delta t\, f$. The [order of accuracy](/anthropocene/learn/computational-physics/derivatives/finite-differences/) of the 3-point Laplacian is 2. With the diffusion number $r$ held fixed, $\Delta t \propto \Delta x^2$, so the time error is also $\Order(\Delta x^2)$. The residual of $\hat u$ in the discrete operator must fall as $\Delta x^2$. If it does not, the code is not the operator you think it is.

The extra `/2` was a coefficient. Hunt the value that brings the slope back.

## Two questions, not one

**Verification:** did you solve the discrete problem you wrote? Residual vs $\Delta x$ at the design order.

**Validation:** is that discrete problem the physics? MMS cannot answer this. A passing order study on the extra `/2` stencil, manufactured for $\alpha = 1/2$, is a verification of $\alpha = 1/2$ heat. It is silent on $\alpha = 1$.

The [CFL](/anthropocene/learn/computational-physics/fields-and-continua/cfl/) lesson already showed you the quiet version of this: first-order upwind at $\nu = 0.4$ is bounded, smooth, and the wrong PDE — advection plus numerical viscosity. Refine. The viscosity vanishes and you recover advection. If the intended physics was heat, refinement never saves you.

<Recall id="vv-solved-right" concept="verification">
  <div slot="front">Verification versus validation, in one line each. What does MMS actually prove?</div>

**Verification:** did I solve the discrete problem I wrote? The test is residual (or error) vs $\Delta x$ at the design order.

**Validation:** is that discrete problem the physics I meant? Comparison to experiment, or to a higher-fidelity description of the intended system.

MMS proves the first, for whatever operator you manufactured against. It cannot prove the second. A pretty picture proves neither.

</Recall>

<Tier level="advanced" title="code verification, solution verification, and what conservation does not buy">

Oberkampf's split inside verification:

- **Code verification.** The software implements the intended discrete operator. MMS plus a grid-convergence study that recovers the design order, with a defined completion point. A theorem-like test: either the slope is $p$, or you have a bug.
- **Solution verification.** *This* run, on *this* mesh, has an estimated numerical error. Richardson / GCI on the quantity of interest. Passing code verification does not certify a coarse production mesh.

Conservation of mass, energy, or $\nabla\!\cdot\! B = 0$ is a **necessary audit**. It catches some bugs and some unstable runs. It does not catch a consistent discretisation of the wrong PDE — the extra `/2` conserves the mean just as well as the right Laplacian. Treat a conservation unit test as a smoke test, not as V&V.

The method of exact solutions (MES) is MMS without the source: you need a genuine closed form of the unforced PDE. Fine when you have one (the sine eigenmode of heat). Useless for a general operator, which is why you manufacture.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

- **Verification** = solved the discrete problem. **Validation** = the discrete problem is the physics.
- The test is residual vs $\Delta x$, at the design order. A pretty field is not a test.
- You can verify the wrong equations perfectly. MMS will help you do it.
