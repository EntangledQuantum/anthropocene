An ODE stored a state vector. A PDE stores a field $u(x)$ — or $u(x,t)$. The
continuum limit is the move from the list to the function.

That field has a question an ODE never asked.

## Poisson does not pick a field

Five samples. You know the three interior second differences: all zero. You do
not know the two endpoints.

$$
(D^2 u)_i = \frac{u_{i-1} - 2u_i + u_{i+1}}{h^2}
$$

So $u_{xx} = f$ on an interval is not an initial-value problem. It is a
boundary-value problem. Pin $u$ at both ends and the discrete Laplacian becomes
invertible. Leave them free and you are looking at a null space.

</Predict>

That is the elliptic personality: **the value at any interior point needs the
whole interval, including both ends.** There is no time. Information does not
travel; it is already everywhere.

## Three shapes

A PDE type is not a discriminant $B^2 - AC$. It is the shape of the data a
point depends on.

- **Hyperbolic** (wave $u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx}$, advection $u_t + c u_x = 0$): a
  triangle. Finite speed.
- **Parabolic** (heat $u_t = \kappa u_{xx}$): the whole past, the instant $t > 0$.
  Infinite speed, exponentially small tails.
- **Elliptic** (Poisson $u_{xx} = f$): the whole interval, always. No time.

## Which wall

Same interval, different costume. Right-going advection

$$
u_t + u_x = 0 \quad \text{on } [0,1]
$$

The solution is $u(x,t) = u(x-t,\,0)$, until the foot of the characteristic
walks off the left end.

The question a PDE type answers: **what information must enter through the
boundary?** For this advection, the left wall. For a 1D wave, both walls (two
families). For heat, both walls and the whole past. For Poisson, both walls and
nothing else — there is no past.

</Predict>

Heat still surprises people who have just drawn a triangle.

The 1D heat kernel on the line is

$$
G(x,t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\kappa t}}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{4\kappa t}\right)
$$

Strictly positive everywhere the instant $t > 0$. Infinite speed. The tails are
not a delay; they are an exponential.

<Recall id="pde-three-pictures" concept="pde">
  <div slot="front">What is the domain of dependence for a 1D wave, for heat, and for Poisson?</div>

Wave: a characteristic triangle of width $2ct$, clipped to the interval. Heat:
the whole past, instantly — a one-sided fill. Poisson: the whole interval,
always, both ends, no time. The type of a PDE is which of those three a point
needs.

</Recall>

<Recall id="pde-needs-boundary" concept="continuum-limit">
  <div slot="front">Why can you not recover a field from its second differences alone?</div>

Interior $D^2$ kills constants and ramps. The kernel is two-dimensional, same
as $u'' = 0$. Poisson is a boundary-value problem: the ends are the missing
data, not a cleanup after the solve. Particles and samples of that field are
the same list; the continuum limit is the list becoming $u(x)$.

</Recall>

A numerical stencil has a domain of dependence too — the grid points a value
can actually see. If that discrete triangle is *smaller* than the PDE's
triangle, the scheme cannot converge: it is missing data the solution depends
on. That mismatch is the CFL condition, and it is the next lesson. Do not
confuse it with the PDE's own triangle. The PDE's triangle is physics. CFL is
whether your stencil covers it.

<Tier level="advanced" title="periodic Poisson is singular until the load sums to zero">

On a ring there are no ends to pin. The periodic discrete Laplacian annihilates
constants, so $D^2 u = f$ has a solution only when $\sum f_i = 0$ — otherwise
you are asking a constant mode to equal something it cannot. Even then the
solution is unique only up to a constant; you still have to pin one value.

That compatibility condition is the discrete divergence theorem: the flux
around a closed loop sums to zero. It is the same null space you just met,
wearing periodic clothes. Dirichlet hid it by deleting the kernel.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

A PDE stores a field. Particles and samples are two costumes of the same list;
the continuum limit is that list becoming $u(x)$.

The type is a picture: triangle, one-sided fill, whole interval. That picture
is what must enter through the boundary.
