A bump on a line, travelling at speed $c$. You already know forward Euler and the centred difference. Combine them — FTCS, the first scheme everyone writes:

```ts
next[i] = u[i] - 0.5 * nu * (u[i + 1] - u[i - 1]);
```

The Courant number $\nu = c\,\Delta t / \Delta x$ is how many cells the bump travels in one step. Set it to $0.5$ — half a cell per step, well inside "information can only come from the neighbours."

## Look the way the wind is blowing

The bump is coming from the left. Sample that neighbour:

```ts
next[i] = u[i] - nu * (u[i] - u[i - 1]);
```

Same $\nu$. Different stencil. Hunt the cliff.

$$
\nu = \frac{c\,\Delta t}{\Delta x}
$$

is how far information travels in one step, **in mesh units**. After $n$ steps a two-point stencil has a domain of dependence $n$ cells wide. The physical one is a triangle of width $c\, n\,\Delta t$. The numerical triangle must contain the physical one. Courant, Friedrichs and Lewy, 1928. That is a necessary condition for convergence. It is not a stability certificate — FTCS just showed you the difference.

<Recall id="cfl-mesh-units" concept="cfl-condition">
  <div slot="front">What is the CFL number, and what does the triangle say you must not do?</div>

$\nu = c\,\Delta t / \Delta x$ is how many mesh cells the signal crosses in one step. The stencil of an explicit scheme only sees its neighbours, so after $n$ steps its domain of dependence is a triangle $n$ cells wide. The true characteristic travels $c\, n\,\Delta t$. If that foot lands outside the triangle, the update is inventing a value and the grid explodes. CFL $\le$ 1 is necessary. It is not sufficient — a centred stencil can see the information and still detonate.

</Recall>

## Same stencil, different units

Heat, $u_t = \alpha u_{xx}$. Same three neighbours, different number:

```ts
next[i] = u[i] + r * (u[i + 1] - 2 * u[i] + u[i - 1]);
```

with $r = \alpha\,\Delta t / \Delta x^2$. The CFL argument in the strict sense never holds — heat fills the whole line instantly, and no explicit stencil contains that domain. Von Neumann still hands you a usable bound.

<Tier level="advanced" title="why centred advection never sits in Euler's disc">

Substitute a mode $u_j^n = G^n e^{i j \theta}$ into FTCS advection. The centred difference brings down $i\sin\theta$, and you get

$$
G = 1 - i\,\nu\sin\theta, \qquad |G| = \sqrt{1 + \nu^2\sin^2\theta} \ge 1.
$$

Equality only at $\theta = 0$. Every other mode grows, for every $\nu \ne 0$. The 4$\Delta x$ mode ($\theta = \pi/2$) is the fastest; the checkerboard is invisible to a centred difference, which is why it is *not* the killer here.

Forward Euler's stability region is the disc $|1 + z| \le 1$ in the $z = h\lambda$ plane. A centred first derivative has a pure-imaginary eigenvalue. That disc does not contain the imaginary axis except at the origin. No $h$ will put the scheme inside.

Upwind adds a real (dissipative) part. The eigenvalues move into the disc — for $\nu \le 1$. The modified equation is the caption:

$$
u_t + c u_x = \tfrac{c\,\Delta x}{2}(1 - \nu)\, u_{xx} + O(\Delta x^2).
$$

At $\nu = 1$ the right-hand side vanishes and the scheme is the shift operator. Below 1 you are solving a viscous problem you did not write down.

Heat FTCS does the opposite: $G = 1 - 4r\sin^2(\theta/2)$, real, and the checkerboard $\theta = \pi$ is the worst, giving $G = 1 - 4r$. That is the $r = 1/2$ bound you just hunted.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

The CFL triangle: information cannot outrun the stencil. $\nu = c\,\Delta t/\Delta x$ is that comparison, in mesh units. Necessary, not sufficient — and a plot that does not explode can still be the scheme pretending to be physics.
