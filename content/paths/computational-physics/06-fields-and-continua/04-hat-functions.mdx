Five equal nodes on $[0,1]$. You already have a stencil for $-u'' = f$:

$$
\frac{-u_{i-1} + 2u_i - u_{i+1}}{h^2} = f_i
$$

Someone else builds a matrix by putting a little tent on each node — value $1$ there, $0$ at every other node, straight lines in between — and asking the residual of $-u''-f$ to be orthogonal to each tent.

## The question you actually asked

The strong form wants $-u'' = f$ at every $x$. A piecewise-linear $u_h$ has $u_h'' = 0$ inside each element, so that sentence is nonsense for the object you can store.

Do not require the residual to vanish at a point. Require it to be orthogonal to a space of test functions $v$:

$$
\int_0^1 u'\, v' \,\mathrm{d}x = \int_0^1 f\, v \,\mathrm{d}x
$$

for every $v$ that vanishes at the ends. Integration by parts moved a derivative onto $v$ and ate the second derivative of $u$. That is the **weak form**. It is equivalent to the strong form for a smooth $u$; it is the form that still makes sense for a tent.

**Galerkin:** use the same hats as both the trial functions (what $u_h$ is made of) and the test functions (what the residual has to be orthogonal to).

$$
u_h = \sum_j u_j\,\varphi_j, \qquad
K_{ij} = \int \varphi_i'\,\varphi_j'\,\mathrm{d}x, \qquad
K\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{F}
$$

The method *is* the inner product you declared small.

## Two 2×2s, overlapping

On one element of length $h$ the two local hats have slopes $\pm 1/h$, so the element matrix is

$$
\frac{1}{h}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}
$$

**Assembly** is: walk the elements, and add each 2×2 into the global slots of the two nodes that element owns. A node that belongs to two elements receives both diagonal contributions.

<Recall id="fem-weak-card" concept="weak-form">
  <div slot="front">What does Galerkin actually require of the residual, and why can a piecewise-linear $u_h$ not satisfy $-u''=f$ pointwise?</div>

Galerkin requires the residual to be **orthogonal to each test function** — here, each hat — not zero at a point.

A piecewise-linear $u_h$ has $u_h'' = 0$ inside every element, so $-u_h'' = f$ is nonsense unless $f$ happens to vanish. The weak form moves a derivative onto the test function,

$$
\int u' v' \,\mathrm{d}x = \int f v \,\mathrm{d}x,
$$

and that sentence still makes sense for a tent. The inner product you pick *is* the method.

</Recall>

<Recall id="fem-hat-card" concept="finite-elements">
  <div slot="front">On a uniform mesh the assembled P1 stiffness is the central-difference stencil. What changes when a neighbour moves, and what does assembly add at the diagonal?</div>

The hat becomes lopsided: slopes $1/h_L$ and $-1/h_R$. The assembled row is

$$
\bigl(-1/h_L,\; 1/h_L + 1/h_R,\; -1/h_R\bigr),
$$

which is $\int \varphi_i'\varphi_j'$, not a skipped neighbour.

Assembly adds overlapping local 2×2s. The diagonal at an interior node is $1/h_L + 1/h_R$ because **two** elements own that node, and both contribute.

</Recall>

<Tier level="advanced" title="why the nodes are exact in 1D, and where the boundary term went">

For $-u''=f$ on an interval, the Green's function $G(\cdot, x_i)$ is itself piecewise linear, with a kink only at $x_i$. If $x_i$ is a mesh node, $G(\cdot, x_i)$ lives in the P1 space. Galerkin orthogonality then forces $u_h(x_i) = u(x_i)$ whenever the load $\int f\,\varphi_i$ is integrated exactly. That is a 1D gift. It does not survive a 2D triangle, and it is not why the method is correct — the method is correct because it asked a well-posed inner-product question. Nodal exactness is a bonus the Green's function paid you.

The boundary term from integration by parts is $[u' v]_0^1$. Test functions vanish at Dirichlet ends, so that term is zero there — those are **essential** conditions, built into the space. A Neumann condition $u'(1) = g$ would have survived as a surface load $g\,v(1)$, imposed weakly, for free, by not constraining $v(1)$. Those are **natural** conditions. The weak form sorts the boundary data: what you put in the space, and what you put on the right-hand side.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

A hat: 1 at its node, 0 at the others, linear in between. Galerkin: the residual is orthogonal to those hats, not zero at a point. Assembly: overlapping local 2×2s, and the diagonal is a sum.

On a uniform mesh that machinery reproduces the stencil you already had. On an irregular mesh it is the reason you still have a matrix, and "skip a neighbour" does not.
