Two cells. The left one holds $1$, the right one holds $0$. Inviscid Burgers. [Finite volume](/learn/computational-physics/fields-and-continua/finite-volume/) already made you write **one** number into both updates. It did not say which number.

The left panel is $(x,t)$. The heavy ray is the shock. The face is the vertical line $x=0$. It never crosses the shock, so it keeps reading $u_L$.

That local initial-value problem — two constant states meeting at a point — is the **Riemann problem**. Its entropy solution is self-similar: $u(x,t)=\hat u(x/t)$. Godunov’s flux is the physical flux of that solution, evaluated at the face:

$$
F_{i+1/2} = f\bigl(\hat u(0;\, Q_i, Q_{i+1})\bigr).
$$

The update is still conservation form. The new sentence is how $F$ is chosen.

<Recall id="rie-card-fan" concept="riemann-problem">
  <div slot="front">What is a Riemann problem, and where does the face sit in its solution?</div>

Two constant states, $u_L$ on the left and $u_R$ on the right, meeting at a point. The entropy solution is a fan of waves, self-similar in $\xi=x/t$: a shock, a rarefaction, or a contact. The face is the ray $\xi=0$. The state there is unique, so the flux is unique.

</Recall>

## Drag until the shock sits on the face

Shock speed for Burgers is $s=(u_L+u_R)/2$. Fix $u_L=1$. Find the $u_R$ that parks the jump on $\xi=0$.

## The other wave is not a jump

$u_L=-1$, $u_R=1$. Rankine–Hugoniot would still give $s=0$. That stationary jump is **illegal**: characteristics run *out* of it. The entropy solution is a rarefaction, $u=\xi$ between the two states.

For a convex flux the cases collapse to one line: Godunov flux is the **minimum** of $f$ between $u_L$ and $u_R$ if $u_L\le u_R$, and the **maximum** if $u_L>u_R$. Burgers has its minimum at $0$, which is why a transonic fan sends $0$.

<Recall id="rie-card-godunov" concept="godunov">
  <div slot="front">What number is the Godunov flux, in one sentence?</div>

The physical flux of the Riemann solution, evaluated at the face: $F_{i+1/2}=f(\hat u(0;Q_i,Q_{i+1}))$. For a shock moving right, that is $f(Q_i)$; for a transonic rarefaction, the sonic value. It is not the average of the two fluxes.

</Recall>

## The same face, three waves

A diaphragm in a tube. Air at rest on both sides; pressure and density higher on the left. The Riemann problem is now a system, and the fan has three waves: a left-going rarefaction, a contact, a right-going shock. Godunov flux is still $\mathbf{f}(\hat U(0))$. Approximate solvers (Roe, HLL, HLLC) exist because the exact Euler answer is an iteration. They answer the same face question more cheaply. Not this lesson.

<Tier level="advanced" title="entropy fixes, and why HLLC is a later costume">

A Roe linearisation of Burgers replaces the rarefaction $-1|1$ by a stationary shock at $s=0$ and sends flux $1/2$. That is an **expansion shock**. An entropy fix (Harten, 1983) spreads the eigenvalue in a neighbourhood of zero so the sonic rarefaction is not collapsed to a jump. Godunov with the exact entropy solution does not need the fix: $\hat u(0)=0$ already.

HLL and HLLC estimate the outermost wave speeds and (for HLLC) restore the contact. They are cheaper answers to the same face question, built because the exact Euler Riemann solver is a Newton iteration on $p_*$. This lesson stops at the exact scalar solver plus the Sod costume. The telescoping from conservation form does not care which $F$ you picked. The profile does.

</Tier>

## What to carry forward

The Riemann fan. Two cells launch a tiny exact problem; the face sits at $\xi=0$ of that solution; Godunov flux is $f(\hat u(0))$. Conservation form said one number, used twice. This is how you pick the number. A slope inside the cell changes the states, not the question.
