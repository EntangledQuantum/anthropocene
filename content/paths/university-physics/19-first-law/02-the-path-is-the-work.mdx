<Step>

## Name it

Work is the area under the path, and heat is whatever the ledger needs besides:

$$W = \int p\,dV \qquad\qquad Q = \Delta U + W$$

Only $\Delta U$ is fixed by the endpoints. $U$ belongs to the state; $Q$ and $W$ belong to the
route.

<Recall id="up-ch19-recall-state" concept="state-function">
  <div slot="front">A gas goes from A to B by two different routes. Which of Q, W and ΔU must be the same both times?</div>

  Only $\Delta U$. Internal energy is a property of the state, so its change needs only the
  two endpoints. $W$ is the area under the route and $Q = \Delta U + W$, so both change with it.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Step>

## Round the loop

Bring the gas back to A and its internal energy is exactly what it was. The work is not.

<WalkThePvPlane
  id="up-ch19-loop"
  loop
  target={1500}
  prompt="Take the gas round a loop back to A, with the gas doing 1500 J of net work."
  explanation="A loop of 1500 J run clockwise: out at high pressure, back at low. Back at A the change in U is 0, so the flame put in 1500 J more than the ice took out, and the piston delivered all of it. That is an engine. Run the same loop anticlockwise and the gas takes in 1500 J of work instead: a refrigerator."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch19-recall-cycle" concept="thermodynamic-cycle">
  <div slot="front">A gas runs round a closed loop on the p–V plane. What is its net work, and where did the energy come from?</div>

  The area inside the loop, positive if it runs clockwise. Round a loop $\Delta U = 0$, so the
  net heat in equals the net work out.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Step>

## Too fast for heat

A bicycle pump with your thumb on the valve. Its barrel is ordinary metal, so heat can leak out,
and the plane below shares its volume axis.

<SlamThePump
  id="up-ch19-slam-the-pump"
  target={430}
  prompt="Squeeze the air to the stop and get it hotter than 430 K."
  explanation="Only a quick stroke, under about 0.85 s, gets there. Heat takes about 1.5 s to leak out, so a fast stroke leaves it no time: Q stays near 0, the work you do stays in the air, and the trace hugs the adiabat, pV^γ = constant. A slow stroke hands the work out through the barrel as it goes and slides toward the isotherm."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch19-recall-adiabatic" concept="adiabatic-process">
  <div slot="front">What makes a compression adiabatic: going slowly, or going fast?</div>

  Going too fast for heat to cross, or having walls that block it. With $Q = 0$, all the work
  goes into $U$, so the gas heats and follows $pV^{\gamma} = \text{const}$.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **The path is the work.** $W$ is the area under the path on the p–V plane, and a loop's net work is the area it encloses.
- **$U$ belongs to the state, $Q$ and $W$ to the route.** A fast squeeze with no time for heat follows the adiabat, steeper than the isotherm.
