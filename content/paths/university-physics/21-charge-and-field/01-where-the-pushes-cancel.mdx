<Step>

## Name it

Each push fell off steeply: halve the distance and it grew fourfold. It also scales with both
charges, and like signs repel while unlike signs attract. That is **Coulomb's law**:

$$F = k\,\dfrac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}, \qquad k = 8.99\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$$

<Recall id="up-ch21-recall-coulomb" concept="coulombs-law">
  <div slot="front">A charge of +9 µC sits 40 cm from a charge of +1 µC. Where between them does a third charge feel no force?</div>

  10 cm from the +1 µC. The +9 must be three times as far, 30 cm, because $3^2 = 9$. The
  balance always sits nearer the smaller charge.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Recall id="up-ch21-recall-conserved" concept="electric-charge">
  <div slot="front">Identical metal spheres carrying +5 µC and −9 µC touch and separate. What does each carry?</div>

  −2 µC each. The total, −4 µC, is conserved and shared equally. Both are now negative,
  so they repel.

## What to carry forward

- **Coulomb's law is an inverse square.** Twice as far, a quarter the force, so balance points sit nearer the smaller charge, and outside the pair when the signs differ.
- **Charge is conserved.** Touching moves it around; the total stays put.
- **Balanced is not stable.** Fixed charges alone cannot hold a charge in place.
