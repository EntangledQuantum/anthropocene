## Where the wavelets cancel

Here is that 2.5 λ slit up close, as twelve sources side by side. Each sends a wavelet toward
the far wall, and the lower it starts, the farther it has to go.

<AimTheDark
  id="up-ch36-aim-the-dark"

  a={2.5}
  start={8}
  explanation="23.6°, the direction of the dark band you put on the mark. There the bottom edge's wavelet goes one whole wavelength farther than the top edge's. At half a wavelength across, the double-slit rule, the loop is only half closed and 40% of the light still arrives."

<Step>

## Name it

A slit is dark wherever its row of wavelets cancels in pairs: a whole number of wavelengths
across the opening.

$$a\sin\theta = m\lambda \qquad (m = 1, 2, 3, \dots)$$

Halve $a$ and $\sin\theta$ doubles. Diffraction is not a new effect: it is interference among
all the sources across one opening.

<Recall id="up-ch36-recall-single-slit" concept="single-slit-diffraction">
  <div slot="front">Green light, 500 nm, passes a slit 2 µm wide. Where is the first dark band, and where is it if the slit closes to 1 µm?</div>

  At 14.5°, since $\sin\theta = \lambda/a = 0.25$. At 1 µm, $\sin\theta = 0.5$, so 30°.
  Close it to 0.5 µm and there is no dark band at all: the light floods the whole half-space.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Narrow the opening, widen the spread.** The first dark band sits at $\sin\theta = \lambda/a$.
- **Diffraction is interference** among the wavelets from every point of one opening, dark where they cancel in pairs.
- **Rays are the wide-opening limit.** When $a$ is thousands of wavelengths, the spread is too small to notice.
