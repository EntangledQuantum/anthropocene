<Step>

## Name it

Each patch of the sleeve contributes the part of the field piercing it times its area. Add every
patch and only the charge inside survives:

$$\Phi_E = \oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A = \frac{Q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$$

Here $d\vec A$ is a patch of surface drawn as an arrow pointing straight out, and for 1 nC inside,
$Q/\varepsilon_0$ is the 113 N·m²/C you have been reading.

<Recall id="up-ch22-recall-flux" concept="electric-flux">
  <div slot="front">A closed surface round a charge is blown up to ten times its size. The field on it is much weaker. Why is the flux unchanged?</div>

  Flux is field times the area it pierces. The surface grows exactly as fast as the field
  weakens, so the product holds, and it equals $Q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0$.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Flux is not the field

A round sleeve 1 m across wraps a +1 rod at its centre. You squash it into a flat oval, still
1 m wide, still round the rod.

<Recall id="up-ch22-recall-gauss" concept="gauss-law">
  <div slot="front">A charge sits just outside a closed surface. The flux through the surface is zero. Is the field on the surface zero?</div>

  No. The field pierces in through the near side and out through the far side, often strongly.
  Only the total cancels.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Flux counts the charge inside**, and nothing else: not the surface's size, shape or position, and not the charges outside.
- **The field is not the flux.** Outside charges reshape it everywhere on the surface.

Next: pick a surface where symmetry makes the field the same all over it, and the flux hands you the field.
