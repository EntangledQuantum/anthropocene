<Step>

## Name it

When a force changes along the way, each short stretch pays its own force times its own
length. Add them up and you get the area under the force line:

$$W = \int_a^b F_x(x)\,dx$$

For a spring pulled by hand, $F = kx$, and that area is a triangle: $W = \tfrac12 kx^2$.

<Recall id="up-ch06-recall-area" concept="variable-force-work">
  <div slot="front">A force changes as a body moves along a line. Where on a graph of force against position is the work it does?</div>

  In the area between the force line and the position axis, from start to finish. Area below
  the axis counts as negative work.
</Recall>

</Step>

## The dip comes first

A 10 kg cart meets a headwind for 2 m, then an equal tailwind for 2 m. The two areas match,
so over the whole track the wind does zero work.

<CartThroughWind
  id="up-ch06-cart-least-launch"
  prompt="Find the slowest launch that still gets the cart to the end of the track."
  explanation="About 2.9 m/s, enough to bring in 40 J. The total is zero, but the cart pays for the headwind first and only collects the tailwind if it gets there. Its kinetic energy follows the running area, and it cannot go below zero."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch06-recall-running-area" concept="variable-force-work">
  <div slot="front">A force does zero total work over a track. Will a cart launched very slowly always get through?</div>

  No. What matters on the way is the running area. If it dips below −½mv₀² before the helpful
  part arrives, the cart stops there and turns back.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Work is the area under force against position,** counted with its sign.
- **The running area is the kinetic-energy account.** A body stops where that account runs
  out, whatever the force does later.

Next: for some forces you can keep that account in advance, and it becomes potential energy.
