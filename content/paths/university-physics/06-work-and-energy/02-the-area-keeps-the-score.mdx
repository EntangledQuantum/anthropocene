## Give each stretch its own rectangle

In [Only the shared piece counts](/anthropocene/learn/university-physics/work-and-energy/only-the-shared-piece-counts/),
you multiplied one constant parallel force by a displacement. Now the height changes:
each short stretch contributes its own $F_x\,\Delta x$. Accumulate those pieces.

$$W_{0\to x}=\int_0^x F_x(s)\,ds.$$

The integral is the **signed area under force versus position**. Its units are newton-metres,
or joules — not square metres. For the straight segments here, triangles and trapezoids give
the area exactly. The dashed energy trace is a separate check: it comes from integrating
Newton's law and measuring the resulting velocity.

The graph gives **net** force along a fixed horizontal track. That makes its work the
change in kinetic energy. If you plotted only one force, its area would be only one entry
in the account.

<Recall id="up-ch6-area-recall-sign" concept="variable-force-work">
  <div slot="front">A cart moves right through a force curve that crosses zero. What does the crossing tell you about accumulated work?</div>

  The work curve has a horizontal tangent there, not necessarily zero height.
  Positive force adds work during the forward passage; negative force subtracts it.
  The score at the crossing includes every earlier signed contribution.
</Recall>

A force graph and an energy graph cannot have arbitrary shapes independently. One gives
the change collected by the other over the next bit of distance. The solid work and dashed
measured energy-change traces should lie almost on top of each other; a tiny residual
reflects numerical integration, not a new kind of energy.

The area beyond the stop is still valid **geometry**. It is not work done on this cart's
forward passage. At the stop, a continuing backward net force would start a return trip;
this distance inspector ends there rather than pretending that moving its slider is a clock.

<Recall id="up-ch6-area-recall-first-stop" concept="variable-force-work">
  <div slot="front">Two force profiles have equal total signed area. What must you check before predicting equal final kinetic energy?</div>

  Check that both forward paths are reachable. Follow the running work from the initial
  kinetic energy and locate the first zero of K. A later positive lobe cannot finance an
  earlier interval the cart cannot cross. Equal endpoint accounts apply only to actual journeys.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

Keep the force curve and the running score together: **height contributes; signed area accumulates**.
Keep the first stop visible: an attractive final sum does not make every intervening point reachable.
Next, reuse that account to read an energy landscape rather than a prescribed force curve.
