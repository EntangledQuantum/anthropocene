<Step>

## Name it

Heat left the iron, the same heat entered the water, and the flow stopped the moment the
temperatures matched. How far each temperature moves is set by the mass and the
**specific heat** $c$: 4186 J/(kg·K) for water, 449 for iron.

$$Q = mc\,\Delta T, \qquad \sum_i m_i c_i\,(T_f - T_i) = 0$$

<Recall id="up-ch17-recall-what-matches" concept="thermal-equilibrium">
  <div slot="front">A hot iron block and cold water stop exchanging heat. What is now equal between them, and what was never equal?</div>

  Their temperatures, and nothing else. The water gained about 32 kJ and the iron lost the
  same 32 kJ; their energies were never equal. **Temperature** is what bodies in contact
  end up sharing. **Heat** is the energy that crossed on the way there.
</Recall>

</Step>

## A degree is a long way up

Rub your hands, brake a bike or churn a bucket, and work becomes warmth. So how much energy
is a single degree?

<Estimate
  id="up-ch17-estimate-lift"
  scenario="up-ch17-lift-for-a-degree"
  prompt="The energy that warms a litre of water by 1 °C could instead lift that same litre straight up. How high?"
  explanation="About 430 m. Warming a kilogram of water by one degree takes 4186 J, and lifting it one metre takes 9.8 J. Joule measured exactly this trade in the 1840s, letting falling weights churn water and watching it warm. A little temperature is a lot of energy."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch17-recall-sparkler" concept="heat">
  <div slot="front">Sparks from a sparkler are hotter than 1000 °C, yet they land on your hand without burning it. Why?</div>

  Each spark has almost no mass, so $Q = mc\,\Delta T$ is tiny even for a huge $\Delta T$.
  It gives up a tiny amount of energy and cools to skin temperature before your skin has warmed
  noticeably. Temperature says which way heat flows; it does not say how much.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Temperature sets the direction of heat; $mc$ sets how far each temperature moves.** Water's large $c$ is why it wins.
- **A mix is an energy budget.** Everything that warms or cools, the pot included, sums to zero.
