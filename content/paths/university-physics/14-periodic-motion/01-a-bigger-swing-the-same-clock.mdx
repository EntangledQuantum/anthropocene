<Step>

## Name it

Pull twice as far and the spring pulls twice as hard, so every part of the motion scales
together. For that, the acceleration has to be proportional to the displacement and point
back toward the middle:

$$a = -\frac{k}{m}\,x = -\omega^2 x,\qquad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$$

That is **simple harmonic motion**, and $A$ appears nowhere in $T$. Only the mass and the
stiffness set the clock.

<Recall id="up-ch14-recall-spring-clock" concept="simple-harmonic-motion">
  <div slot="front">A cart on a spring is pulled twice as far. Why does one cycle still take the same time?</div>

  Its acceleration is proportional to its displacement, so twice the pull means twice the
  acceleration and twice the speed at every moment. Twice the distance at twice the speed
  takes the same time. Only $m/k$ sets the period: four times the mass doubles it.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Step>

## Name the break

The pendulum's pull back toward the bottom is proportional to $\sin\theta$, not $\theta$.
For small angles the two are almost equal, and the pendulum becomes the spring clock:

$$\ddot\theta = -\frac{g}{L}\sin\theta \;\approx\; -\frac{g}{L}\,\theta,\qquad T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$

At larger angles $\sin\theta$ falls short of $\theta$, the pull back is weaker than the
clock assumes, and every swing takes longer.

<Recall id="up-ch14-recall-pendulum-limit" concept="pendulum-small-angle">
  <div slot="front">Why does a pendulum released from 60° run slow against the small-angle clock?</div>

  Its restoring pull goes as $\sin\theta$, which is smaller than $\theta$ at large angles. The
  pull back is weaker than the clock assumes, so it takes longer to return: 7% longer at
  60°. The small-angle period $2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ is only a limit.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Which clocks keep time?

The test is always the same one: does the pull back grow in proportion to how far out the
object is?

## What to carry forward

- **A proportional pull back makes a clock that ignores size.** Twice the swing is twice
  the speed, so the period is the same.
- **A pendulum is that clock only at small angles.** Its period grows as the swing grows.

Next: a swing slowly loses energy, and you have to know exactly when to push to put some
back.
