## Name it

Where $E$ is below $U$, the Schrödinger equation bends the wave away from the axis instead of back, so it decays instead of swinging:

$$\psi \propto e^{-\kappa x},\qquad \kappa = \frac{\sqrt{2m(U-E)}}{\hbar},\qquad T \approx e^{-2\kappa a}$$

Chapter 7's turning point is where the swinging stops and the decay begins.

<Recall id="up-ch40-recall-thicker" concept="quantum-tunnelling">
  <div slot="front">A barrier lets 1% through. Make it twice as thick. Roughly how much gets through now?</div>

  Far less than half: each thickness multiplies what gets through by about $e^{-2\kappa a}$, so two
  of them multiply it twice. For a 1 eV electron and a 2 eV wall, 1% becomes 0.0025%.
</Recall>

</Step>

## One atom higher

A scanning tunnelling microscope holds a metal tip a fraction of a nanometre above a surface. Electrons cross the gap through a barrier 4.5 eV above their energy, and the current counts how many get through.

Alpha decay is the same leak from inside a nucleus, which is why half-lives range from microseconds to billions of years."

## What to carry forward

- **Past the turning point a wave decays; it does not stop.** Whatever is left at the far side carries on with the energy it came in with.
- **What gets through falls exponentially with thickness**, $T \approx e^{-2\kappa a}$: a little more wall, a lot less ghost.
