<Step>

## Name it

You doubled the push two ways: faster molecules, or the same molecules crowded into half
the room. Pressure is the momentum the walls receive per second per square metre, and
counting it for $N$ molecules gives

$$PV = NkT$$

with $T$ in kelvin. Chemists count molecules in moles and write $PV = nRT$, where
$R = N_A k = 8.314$ J/(mol·K).

<Recall id="up-ch18-recall-kelvin" concept="ideal-gas-law">
  <div slot="front">Why does the ideal gas law need kelvin rather than degrees Celsius?</div>

  The push comes from molecular motion, and the motion stops at 0 K, not at 0 °C. Only a
  scale that starts where the pushing stops can double when the push doubles. 20 °C to
  40 °C is 293 K to 313 K: under 7%.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Recall id="up-ch18-recall-hits" concept="kinetic-pressure">
  <div slot="front">Heating a sealed gas raises its pressure. Do the molecules hit the wall more often, or harder?</div>

  Both. Faster molecules reach the wall sooner and bounce off with more momentum. Each
  factor goes as the speed, so the pressure goes as speed squared, which is as the kelvin
  temperature.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Pressure is momentum rain:** hits per second times momentum per hit, per area of wall.
- **$PV = NkT$ counts in kelvin,** and holds only while the molecules are tiny next to the room.
