## Name it

The energy meter never moved: collisions shuffle kinetic energy between molecules but keep
the total, and the average per molecule is what a thermometer reads. In the room, where
molecules move in three directions,

$$\left\langle \tfrac12 mv^2 \right\rangle = \tfrac32 kT$$

The hump is the **Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution**, and one temperature always makes the
same hump, whatever the start.

<Recall id="up-ch18-recall-vrms" concept="maxwell-boltzmann-distribution">
  <div slot="front">Nitrogen at 20 °C has an rms speed of 511 m/s. Is that how fast a typical molecule moves?</div>

  No. It is an average over a wide spread. Most molecules are slower, the commonest speed is
  lower still, and a thin tail runs far above it. A thermometer reads the average energy,
  not anyone's speed.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Temperature is average kinetic energy per molecule,** the same for every kind of molecule in a mixture.
- **No molecule moves at "the" speed.** Collisions spread any start into the Maxwell–Boltzmann hump, with a long fast tail.
- The picture: a lopsided histogram of speeds that fattens and slides right as you heat the gas.

Next: a gas's energy has a ledger, and heat and work are the two ways it talks to the outside.
