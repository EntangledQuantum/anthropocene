## Name it

The lever set how steep the height trace was. The height was the area piled up under the lever's trace. Those are the two halves of calculus, and you have done both by hand:

$$
v = \frac{dx}{dt} \qquad\qquad \Delta x = \int v\,dt
$$

<Recall id="up-ch02-recall-slope" concept="instantaneous-rate">
  <div slot="front">On a trace of height against time, where do you read the height, and where do you read the speed?</div>

  The height is how high the trace sits. The speed is how steep it is at that instant: the
  tangent. They are unrelated, and the highest point of a trip is where the trace is flat.
</Recall>

<Recall id="up-ch02-recall-missing-start" concept="accumulation">
  <div slot="front">You have only a lift's lever history, $v(t)$. What can you work out, and what is missing?</div>

  The change in height: the signed area under $v(t)$. The starting floor is missing. Two
  lifts with the same lever history end up the same distance from where each began, and
  nothing in $v(t)$ says where that was.
</Recall>

</Step>

## What to carry forward

- **One trip, one trace.** Speed is how steep the trace is at an instant. Height is how high it sits.
- **Distance piles up as area under the velocity,** with a sign. Where you started is never in the velocity history.
