## Name it

The speed came down as a straight line, because the brakes take off the same amount every second. The distance is the area under that line, a triangle $v$ tall and $v/a$ long:

$$
d = \tfrac12 \cdot v \cdot \frac{v}{a} = \frac{v^2}{2a} \qquad\text{and, from any start,}\qquad v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\,\Delta x
$$

<Recall id="up-ch02-recall-square" concept="constant-acceleration">
  <div slot="front">Braking just as hard, you arrive 1.5 times faster than usual. How much more road do you need to stop?</div>

  $1.5^2 = 2.25$ times as much. The stop is the triangle under $v(t)$, and both its height
  and its length grow with the speed.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Recall id="up-ch02-recall-top" concept="free-fall">
  <div slot="front">A ball thrown straight up is at the top of its flight. What are its velocity and its acceleration?</div>

  Velocity zero. Acceleration $g$, 9.81 m/s² downward, the same as at every other moment
  of the flight. Zero velocity says nothing about how the velocity is changing.
</Recall>

## Where the triangle lies

Every line of this lesson assumed one thing: the acceleration never changes.

## What to carry forward

- **Constant acceleration makes v(t) a straight line,** and the distance is the triangle under it, so it grows as the square of the speed.
- **The top of a throw is at rest and still accelerating.** Nothing happens there except that the velocity passes through zero.
- The equations belong to **one fixed a**. Check that before you use them.
