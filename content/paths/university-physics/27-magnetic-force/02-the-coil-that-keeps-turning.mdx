<Step>

## Name it

Each long side is a straight wire of length $L$ in the field, pushed by $\vec F = I\vec L\times\vec B$:
here 20 turns × 2 A × 0.05 m × 0.5 T = 1.0 N. Their torque about the axle is

$$\tau = NIAB\sin\theta = |\vec\mu\times\vec B|, \qquad \vec\mu = NIA\,\hat n$$

The coil acts as a magnetic moment $\vec\mu$ out of its face, and the field turns $\vec\mu$ toward
$\vec B$. The net force is zero, as the meter showed.

<Recall id="up-ch27-recall-torque" concept="magnetic-dipole-moment">
  <div slot="front">A current loop sits in a uniform field with its face square to the field. What torque and what net force does it feel?</div>

  Neither. Its moment $\vec\mu$ points along $\vec B$, so $\tau = \mu B\sin 0 = 0$, and in a
  uniform field the forces on opposite sides always cancel. It is at rest, and stably.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Recall id="up-ch27-recall-wire" concept="magnetic-force-on-current">
  <div slot="front">A copper wire carries current but has no net charge. Why does a magnetic field push it?</div>

  The field pushes moving charge, not net charge. The wire's electrons drift and its ions
  do not, so the electrons are pushed sideways and drag the wire with them: $\vec F = I\vec L\times\vec B$.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **A current in a field is pushed sideways:** $\vec F = I\vec L\times\vec B$.
- **A loop feels a torque, not a net force:** $\tau = NIAB\sin\theta$, turning $\vec\mu$ toward $\vec B$, and a motor reverses the current each half turn to keep it going.
