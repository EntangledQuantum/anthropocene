## Name it

The push grew with the field and stayed at right angles to the motion. That push is how the
**magnetic field** is defined:

$$\vec F = q\,\vec v\times\vec B, \qquad F = |q|\,vB \text{ when } \vec v \perp \vec B$$

It is always perpendicular to $\vec v$, so it does no work and the speed never changes. As
the push toward the centre it sets the circle: $|q|vB = mv^2/r$, so $r = mv/(|q|B)$.

<Recall id="up-ch27-recall-no-work" concept="magnetic-force-on-charge">
  <div slot="front">Why can a steady magnetic field never speed up a charged particle?</div>

  Its push $q\vec v\times\vec B$ is always at right angles to the velocity, so it does no
  work: $\vec F\cdot\vec v = 0$. It turns the velocity and leaves the speed alone.
</Recall>

<Recall id="up-ch27-recall-at-rest" concept="magnetic-field">
  <div slot="front">A proton sits at rest between the poles of a strong magnet. What force does the field exert on it?</div>

  None. The magnetic force is $q\vec v\times\vec B$, and with $\vec v = 0$ it vanishes. A
  magnetic field only acts on charge that moves.
</Recall>

<Recall id="up-ch27-recall-lap" concept="cyclotron-motion">
  <div slot="front">A proton's speed doubles in a uniform field. What happens to its circle and its lap time?</div>

  The radius $r = mv/(|q|B)$ doubles; the lap time $T = 2\pi m/(|q|B)$ stays the same. This
  is why a cyclotron can kick its particles at one fixed rhythm as they speed up.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **The magnetic push is sideways.** $\vec F = q\vec v\times\vec B$ turns a velocity and never changes its speed.
- **Stronger field, tighter circle:** $r = mv/(|q|B)$, and the lap time does not care about speed.
- **Mind the sign.** The right-hand rule gives $\vec v\times\vec B$; a negative charge is pushed the other way.
