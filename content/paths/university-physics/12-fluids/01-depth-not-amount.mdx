## Name it

Every metre down adds the weight of a metre of water on each square metre, and sideways steps
add nothing. That is why the shape of the vessel never entered:

$$p = p_0 + \rho g h$$

Here $h$ is the depth below the free surface, and the pressure pushes every way at once: into
the probe from all sides, down on a floor, up on a roof.

<Recall id="up-ch12-recall-depth" concept="hydrostatic-pressure">
  <div slot="front">A dam holds back a lake 10 km long. A lock beside it holds a strip of water 3 m wide, just as deep. Which wall is pushed harder per square metre?</div>

  Neither. The pressure at each depth is $\rho g h$, whatever stretches out behind the wall.
  A wall only feels the water touching it.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Squeezed from every side

Hold a cube under water and the water pushes on all six faces. The deeper faces are pushed
harder, so the pushes do not cancel.

<HoldItUnder
  id="up-ch12-stops-growing"
  prompt="Find the shallowest depth at which the water's net push stops growing."
  explanation="The moment the top face goes under: 628 N, and not a newton more however deep you take it. Every arrow keeps growing, but the bottom always sits 0.4 m below the top, so the difference is always ρg × 0.4 m on each square metre. That difference is buoyancy, and it equals the weight of the water that would fill the cube's place."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch12-recall-archimedes" concept="buoyant-force">
  <div slot="front">A 1-litre steel ball and a 1-litre wooden ball are both held fully under water. Which does the water push up on harder?</div>

  Neither: each gets 9.8 N, the weight of the litre of water it replaces. The water only
  feels the shape it presses on. The steel sinks because it weighs 77 N, not because it is
  pushed less.
</Recall>

## Load it to the mark

A floating hull settles where the water it shoves aside weighs as much as it does:
$F_B = \rho g V_{\text{sub}}$. This deck is 10 m by 4 m, so every tonne you load pushes aside
one more cubic metre.

<LoadTheBarge
  id="up-ch12-barge-to-mark"
  prompt="Load crates, then cast off. Settle the waterline on the mark."
  mark={1.1}
  explanation="Twelve crates, 24 t. At 1.1 m the hull pushes aside 10 × 4 × 1.1 = 44 m³ of water, which weighs exactly as much as the barge and its cargo. The pressure on the floor, ρg × 1.1 m across 40 m², is that same 432 kN. A steel hull floats because it can push aside its own weight in water before the deck goes under."
/>

## What to carry forward

- **Pressure is depth.** $\rho g h$ below the free surface, pushing every way, whatever the vessel's shape.
- **Buoyancy is the bottom's push minus the top's.** It equals the weight of the water pushed aside, and it stops growing once the body is under.
