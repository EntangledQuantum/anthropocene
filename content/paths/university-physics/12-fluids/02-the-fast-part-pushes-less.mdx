## Twice as fast

The pump delivers the same volume every second, whatever you do to the waist. The waist's
speedometer is covered.

## Name it

The volume per second is the same through every section, and a parcel only speeds up when the
pressure behind it beats the pressure ahead. Along one streamline of a steady, frictionless
flow, those two facts make an energy budget:

$$A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2 \qquad\qquad p + \tfrac12\rho v^2 + \rho g y = \text{constant}$$

<Recall id="up-ch12-recall-bernoulli" concept="bernoulli-equation">
  <div slot="front">Why must the water in a pipe's narrow waist be at lower pressure than the water before it?</div>

  It is faster there, so it sped up on the way in, and speeding up needs a net push forward.
  Only the surrounding water pushes on it, so the pressure behind each parcel must beat the
  pressure ahead. Fast water is water whose pressure has been spent.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Aim the jet

Open a hole in a tank and the budget runs from the still surface to the jet. Both are at the
air's pressure, so the depth alone pays for the speed, $v = \sqrt{2gh}$, and after that the
water is a projectile.

<PunchTheHole
  id="up-ch12-hit-the-drain"
  prompt="Place the hole, pull the plug, and land the jet in the drain."
  drain={0.8}
  explanation="From 0.20 m up, or from 0.80 m. The low hole is twice as fast, 3.96 m/s against 1.98 m/s, but it has a quarter of the height to fall, so it spends half as long in the air. The two cancel exactly: the range is 2√(y(H − y)), the same from either side of halfway, and farthest, 1.0 m, from halfway down."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch12-recall-torricelli" concept="torricelli-efflux">
  <div slot="front">Why does a hole at the very bottom of a tank not throw its jet the farthest across the floor?</div>

  It is the fastest jet, at $\sqrt{2gH}$, but it starts almost on the floor and lands almost
  at once. Reach needs speed and time in the air together, and the best trade is from
  halfway down.
</Recall>

## Where Bernoulli lies

The budget holds along one streamline of a flow that is steady, frictionless and
incompressible. Break one of those and it can point the wrong way.

## What to carry forward

- **The same volume every second through every section.** Squeeze the area and the speed rises in proportion, and area goes as width squared.
- **Speed costs pressure.** Along one steady streamline, $p + \tfrac12\rho v^2 + \rho g y$ holds, so fast water is low-pressure water and a jet's speed is paid for by depth.
