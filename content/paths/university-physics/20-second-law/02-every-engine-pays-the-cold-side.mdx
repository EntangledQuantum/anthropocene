## Name it

Heat $Q$ leaving or entering a bath at temperature $T$ carries entropy $Q/T$, and work
carries none. The engine ends each cycle as it began, so the baths are the whole ledger, and
the total may not fall:

$$\frac{Q_c}{T_c} \ge \frac{Q_h}{T_h} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \eta = \frac{W}{Q_h} \le 1 - \frac{T_c}{T_h}$$

Only the two temperatures enter, in kelvin: that is the Carnot limit, and the engine that
reaches it adds no entropy at all.

<Recall id="up-ch20-recall-carnot" concept="carnot-efficiency">
  <div slot="front">Steam at 300 °C, a river at 20 °C. What is the best efficiency any engine between them could reach?</div>

  $1 - 293/573 = 49\%$. The ratio needs kelvin: in Celsius you would get a wrong 93%.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Heat carries entropy $Q/T$; work carries none.** The total never falls.
- **Every engine pays the cold side** at least $Q_h T_c/T_h$, so no engine beats $1 - T_c/T_h$.
- **A fridge is the same ledger run backwards**, paid for with work.
