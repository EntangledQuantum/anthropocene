<Step>

## Name it

In the free expansion no heat went in, no work was done and the energy did not change. What
grew is the count: each particle has twice the room, so the arrangements multiply by $2^N$.
Entropy is the log of that count,

$$S = k_B \ln W, \qquad \Delta S_{\text{free expansion}} = N k_B \ln 2$$

<Recall id="up-ch20-recall-what-rose" concept="statistical-entropy">
  <div slot="front">A gas doubles its volume into a vacuum: Q = 0, W = 0, ΔU = 0. What rose, and by how much for one mole?</div>

  The number of arrangements, by a factor $2^N$. Its log, the entropy, rose by
  $N k_B \ln 2$, which for a mole is $R \ln 2 = 5.76$ J/K.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Step>

## How many is never?

With $N$ particles, a random look catches them all on the left once in $2^N$ looks.

<Estimate
  id="up-ch20-estimate-universe"
  scenario="up-ch20-universe-wait"
  prompt="You look at the box once a second, and the gas reshuffles between looks. How many particles does it take before you expect to wait longer than the age of the universe to catch them all on the left?"
  explanation="About 59. You need about 2^N looks, and 2^59 seconds is 18 billion years. A cubic centimetre of air holds 2.5 × 10¹⁹ molecules. Nothing forbids the return; the count alone makes it never."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch20-recall-never" concept="irreversibility">
  <div slot="front">The motion of every molecule runs just as well backwards. So why does a spread gas never gather again?</div>

  Gathered is one arrangement; spread is almost all of the $2^N$. The gas visits every
  arrangement equally, so it spends essentially all its time spread.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Irreversible means outnumbered, not forbidden.** All-left is one arrangement of $2^N$.
- **Entropy is a count, logged:** $S = k_B \ln W$. Doubling the room adds $N k_B \ln 2$.

Next: heat carries entropy too, and that is why every engine has to pay the cold side.
