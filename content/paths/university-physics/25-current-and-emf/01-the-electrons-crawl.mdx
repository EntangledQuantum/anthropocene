## Name it

Current is the charge crossing the wire each second. It is the number of carriers per cubic metre, times the charge each carries, times the area they cross, times their drift speed:

$$I = n\,q\,A\,v_d$$

Copper's $n$ is so large that $v_d$ comes out as a crawl for any current you will meet.

<Recall id="up-ch25-recall-instant-lamp" concept="drift-velocity">
  <div slot="front">If the electrons drift at a fraction of a millimetre per second, why does a lamp light the instant you flip the switch?</div>

  The wire is already full of electrons, including those in the filament. The switch sets up a
  field along the whole loop in nanoseconds, and every electron starts to drift at once. The
  push travels fast; the charge does not.
</Recall>

<Recall id="up-ch25-recall-drawn-wire" concept="resistance">
  <div slot="front">You draw a copper wire out to twice its length, keeping the same amount of metal. What happens to its resistance?</div>

  It becomes four times as large. Twice the length doubles $R = \rho L/A$, and the same metal
  spread over twice the length has half the cross-section, which doubles it again.

## What to carry forward

- **Current is a crowd creeping together.** $I = nqAv_d$; in copper the drift is a fraction of a millimetre per second.
- **The switch-on push is a different object.** It crosses the wire in nanoseconds and brings the energy with it.
- **Resistance is shape times metal.** $R = \rho L/A$: longer resists more, thicker less.
