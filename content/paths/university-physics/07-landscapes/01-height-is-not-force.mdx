## Flat, not low

A cart at rest stays at rest only where nothing pushes it along the rail.

<Step>

## Steep pushes, high does not

Here gravity's potential energy is $U = mgh$, so the track *is* the energy landscape. Measure $x$ along the rail, and the push is the slope of that landscape, turned downhill:

$$F = -\frac{dU}{dx}$$

<Recall id="up-ch07-recall-slope" concept="potential-force-slope">
  <div slot="front">A high flat shelf and a low steep slope: where is the push stronger, and why?</div>

  On the steep slope. Force is minus the slope of U, so a flat stretch pushes with zero force at
  any height. Read the tilt, not the altitude.
</Recall>

</Step>

So far every height was measured from the floor. Measure from a tabletop 0.8 m up instead, and the cart's potential energy on the valley floor becomes −11.8 J.

## What to carry forward

- **Force reads the slope of U, never its height.** $F = -dU/dx$: flat means no push, at any altitude.
- **The zero of U is yours.** Only differences and slopes reach the world.
