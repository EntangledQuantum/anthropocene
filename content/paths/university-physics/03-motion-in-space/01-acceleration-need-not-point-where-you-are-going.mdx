## Name it

Holding a circle takes an acceleration pointing at its centre, of size

$$
a = \frac{v^2}{R}
$$

That is **uniform circular motion**: the speed is constant, the velocity is not, and the pull toward the centre is what turns it.

<Recall id="up-ch03-recall-centripetal" concept="centripetal-acceleration">
  <div slot="front">You take the same roundabout at three times the speed. How much harder must the tyres pull the car sideways?</div>

  Nine times. The acceleration is $v^2/R$, so it goes with the square of the speed.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Let go

## What to carry forward

- **One arrow, two jobs.** The part of the acceleration along the velocity changes the speed; the part across it turns the velocity.
- **A circle is pure turning.** It needs $v^2/R$ toward the centre: twice as fast takes four times the pull. Remove the pull and the path goes straight.

Next: pin the arrow to the ground and point it straight down, and a thrown ball becomes two simple motions sharing one clock.
