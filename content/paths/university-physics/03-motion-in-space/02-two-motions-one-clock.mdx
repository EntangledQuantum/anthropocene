## Name it

Sideways nothing acts, so $x = v_0 t$ and the strobe marks stay evenly spaced. Downward gravity acts alone, so $y = h - \tfrac12 g t^2$, the dropped ball's fall exactly. The two stories share one thing: the clock $t$.

<Recall id="up-ch03-recall-independence" concept="projectile-independence">
  <div slot="front">You throw a ball sideways off a cliff, then throw another twice as hard. What changes: the time in the air, the distance, or both?</div>

  Only the distance, which doubles. The time is set by the fall alone, and the fall is the
  same.
</Recall>

## Put the air back

Now two ping-pong balls leave a 3 m balcony, one pushed at 12 m/s. Air pushes back on each ball, harder the faster it moves, and a ping-pong ball's terminal speed is only 8.7 m/s.

<Recall id="up-ch03-recall-terminal" concept="terminal-velocity-preview">
  <div slot="front">When can you trust the drag-free parabola for a thrown ball?</div>

  When the ball stays well below its terminal speed. Near that speed drag is as strong as
  gravity, and because it depends on the whole speed it ties the sideways and falling
  motions together.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **A throw is two motions on one clock.** The fall sets the time; the sideways speed decides how far you get in it.
- **Gravity drops everything by the same ½gt²**, which is why aiming straight at a falling target works.
- **Air breaks the split** once the speed is anywhere near terminal speed.
