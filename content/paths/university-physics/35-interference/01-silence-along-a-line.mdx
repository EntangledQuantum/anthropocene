<Step>

## Name it

Loud or still is decided by one number: how much farther one wave travels than the other,
the path difference $\Delta r = r_2 - r_1$.

$$\Delta r = m\lambda \;\;\text{loud},\qquad \Delta r = \left(m+\tfrac12\right)\lambda \;\;\text{still}$$

A whole wavelength late is back in step: the crest that arrives is just the next one.

<Recall id="up-ch35-recall-whole-lambda" concept="path-difference">
  <div slot="front">A spot is exactly one wavelength farther from one source than the other. Is it quiet?</div>

  No, it is as loud as anywhere. One whole wavelength of delay brings a crest in with a crest.
  Only a half-integer difference puts a crest on a trough.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Two motors, no pattern

Two ordinary lamps, or two separate motors, never stay exactly in step. Suppose the upper
dipper runs a tenth of a hertz fast.

<Recall id="up-ch35-recall-coherence" concept="coherence">
  <div slot="front">Why do two ordinary lamps side by side not paint stripes on a wall?</div>

  Their timing drifts at random, millions of times a second, so the quiet lines sweep away
  faster than any eye can follow. A steady pattern needs sources that stay in step.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Path difference decides it.** Whole wavelengths stack; half-integers cancel, along lines.
- **The sources must stay in step.** Drift, and the pattern sweeps away into the plain sum.
