<Step>

## Land the stripes

Two marks sit on the far wall, 6 cm either side of centre. The wall slides.

<LandTheFringes
  id="up-ch35-land-fringes"
  prompt="Drag the far wall until the first loud stripes land on the marks."
  explanation="The stripes run out from the gaps along straight lines, so twice as far is twice as wide. Each loud line is a fixed path difference, one wavelength for the first."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch35-recall-energy" concept="double-slit">
  <div slot="front">A loud stripe is four times as bright as one gap alone, a dark stripe zero. Was light destroyed?</div>

  No, it was moved. Averaged over the wall, two gaps give exactly twice one gap. Interference
  takes energy out of the dark stripes and puts it into the bright ones.
</Recall>

<Step>

## Name it: Young's formula

For a far wall, the $m$-th loud stripe sits at $y_m \approx m\lambda L/d$, so neighbours
are evenly spaced:

$$\Delta y = \frac{\lambda L}{d}$$

In 1801 Young read it backwards and measured a wavelength of light.

<Estimate
  id="up-ch35-laser-wavelength"
  scenario="up-ch35-laser"
  prompt="A laser lights two slits 0.25 mm apart. On a wall 3.0 m away its bright stripes are 7.6 mm apart. What is its wavelength?"
  explanation="633 nm, a helium-neon laser: λ = Δy·d/L = 7.6 mm × 0.25 mm ÷ 3.0 m. The wavelength is so small that it takes metres of lever arm to turn it into millimetres you can measure with a ruler."
/>

</Step>

## A bubble goes black

A soap film in white light shows bands of colour, set by its thickness. As it drains, the
top gets thinner than any wavelength, just before it pops.

<DrainTheFilm
  id="up-ch35-drain-film"
  prompt="Thin the film from the front until its top reflects no light."
  explanation="Far thinner than a wavelength, the trip through the film adds almost nothing. But the front reflection, off the denser soap, comes back flipped; the back one, off air, does not. Crest meets trough for every colour at once, so the top goes black. The purple band near 200 nm only cancels green."
/>

</Step>

<Step>

## The flip is not optional

Light reflecting off a denser medium comes back flipped by half a wave; off a thinner one,
it does not. A soap film in air has one flip, so for a film of index $n$ and thickness $t$:

$$2nt = m\lambda\;\;\text{dark},\qquad 2nt = \left(m+\tfrac12\right)\lambda\;\;\text{bright}$$

<Recall id="up-ch35-recall-flip" concept="thin-film-interference">
  <div slot="front">The thinnest film: both reflections travel almost the same distance. Why is it dark rather than bright?</div>

  The front reflection, off the denser soap, is flipped by half a wave; the back one, off
  air, is not. Equal paths plus one flip is crest on trough.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Stripes are path difference made visible**: $\Delta y = \lambda L/d$, a ruler for wavelengths.
- **Dark is light moved, not lost.** Block one wave and a dark spot lights up.
- **Count the flips.** A soap film's one flip makes its thinnest part black.
