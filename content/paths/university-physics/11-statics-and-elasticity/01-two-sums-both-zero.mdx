## Name it

The seesaw needed two things at once. The pivot's push made the forces add to zero; the
arms made the turning effects add to zero.

$$\sum \vec F = 0 \qquad\qquad \sum \tau = 0$$

Push a plank on ice up at one end and down at the other, equally hard: it passes the first
test, fails the second, and spins in place.

<Recall id="up-ch11-recall-two-sums" concept="static-equilibrium">
  <div slot="front">Two equal and opposite forces act on a resting plank, at different points. Does it stay at rest?</div>

  No. The forces add to zero, so its centre stays put, but their torques do not cancel. It
  spins in place. Rest needs both sums to vanish.
</Recall>

</Step>

## What to carry forward

- **Rest takes two sums.** Forces add to zero *and* torques add to zero. A couple passes the first and spins anyway.
- **Take torques about any point.** Put it on an unknown's line of action and that unknown drops out.
- **A support pushes; it never pulls.** Tipping is the moment one push reaches zero.
