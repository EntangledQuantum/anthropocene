<Step>

## Name it

Force per area is **stress**. Stretch per length is **strain**. Below the limit one is a fixed
multiple of the other, and that multiple, Young's modulus, is the material's stiffness.

$$\sigma = \frac{F}{A} \qquad \varepsilon = \frac{\Delta L}{L} \qquad \sigma = E\,\varepsilon$$

<Recall id="up-ch11-recall-strain" concept="strain">
  <div slot="front">A 100 m steel cable and a 1 m sample of the same steel carry the same stress. Which stretches more?</div>

  The cable, by 100 times as many millimetres: every metre stretches its share. The strain,
  stretch over length, is the same for both. That is the number that belongs to the steel.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Recall id="up-ch11-recall-limit" concept="elastic-limit">
  <div slot="front">You bend a paper clip open and let go. Why does it stay open?</div>

  You pushed the metal past its elastic limit. Past that point it flows, and when the load
  comes off it springs back only along a line parallel to the one it went up: most of the
  bend stays.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **A material feels stress, not load.** Twice the diameter, a quarter of the stress.
- **It answers with strain, not millimetres.** Below the limit, $\sigma = E\varepsilon$.
- **Past the elastic limit it flows and keeps the stretch.** Stiff is the slope; strong is where the line ends.
