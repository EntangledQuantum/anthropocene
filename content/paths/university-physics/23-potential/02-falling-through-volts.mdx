<Step>

## Name it

The field's work came out of the height the charge fell through, so kinetic energy plus $qV$
stayed fixed, as on chapter 7's energy line:

$$\Delta K = -q\,\Delta V$$

One elementary charge through one volt gains one **electron-volt**, $1\ \text{eV} = 1.6\times10^{-19}$ J:
a unit of energy, not of voltage.

<Recall id="up-ch23-recall-ev" concept="electron-volt">
  <div slot="front">An alpha particle, charge +2e, falls from rest through 5 kV. What is its kinetic energy?</div>

  10 keV. Charge times voltage: 2 elementary charges times 5000 V. In joules that is
  $1.6\times10^{-15}$ J, and the alpha's mass never enters.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Where the ground is steepest

Between the plates the contours were evenly spaced, so the field was the same everywhere:
$E = \Delta V/d$. Around charges the spacing changes from place to place.

<FindTheSteepSpot
  id="up-ch23-steepest-spot"

  explanation="Beside the −3 nC, where V is lowest and the contours are packed tightest: about 42 kV/m, against 15 kV/m on the 880 V high ground near the +8 nC. The field reads the steepness, not the height. Air breaks down near 3 MV/m, so a spark starts wherever contours crowd first, which is why sharp points spark."

<Recall id="up-ch23-recall-gap" concept="field-from-potential">
  <div slot="front">Two plates 2 mm apart are held 600 V apart. What is the field between them?</div>

  $3\times10^{5}$ V/m. In a uniform gap the potential falls steadily, so $E = \Delta V/d = 600\ \text{V} / 0.002\ \text{m}$.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **ΔK = −qΔV.** Falling through a voltage gives charge × voltage of energy; the gap and the mass change only the time and the speed.
- **The electron-volt is an energy**: one elementary charge through one volt, $1.6\times10^{-19}$ J.
- **The field is the steepness, not the height**: strongest where the contours crowd.
