## A clock made of light

The simplest clock there is: light bouncing between two mirrors, one tick per round trip.
One stands on the platform and one rides the train, and both frames measure the light at $c$.

  id="up-ch37-light-clock"

  explanation="0.866c. Seen from the platform, the train's light runs a slanted path twice the mirror gap at the same speed c, so each tick takes twice as long. Nothing is wrong with the train's clock: a passenger watching it sees the light go straight up and down, as usual."
/>

<Step>

## Name it

Each leg of the zig-zag is a right triangle: the light goes $ct$ along the slant while it
climbs the gap $L$ and the train slides $vt$.

$$(ct)^2 = L^2 + (vt)^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t = \frac{L/c}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}, \qquad \Delta t = \gamma\,\Delta\tau, \quad \gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$$

$\Delta\tau$ is the **proper time** on the moving clock itself. Physics is the same in every
frame, so every clock slows by the same $\gamma$: a muon's decay clock included.

<Recall id="up-ch37-recall-dilation" concept="time-dilation">
  <div slot="front">Why must a light clock moving past you tick slower than yours?</div>

  Its light runs a slanted zig-zag, longer than the straight up-and-down, and still goes at
  $c$ for you. A longer path at the same speed takes longer: by exactly
  $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$, from Pythagoras on one leg.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Ride with the muon

In its own frame, that slowest muon sits still with a clock running normally, and the
ground rushes up at 0.99904c.

<Recall id="up-ch37-recall-contraction" concept="length-contraction">
  <div slot="front">In the muon's own frame its clock runs normally. So how does it cross 15 km of air in 2.2 µs?</div>

  In its frame the air is moving, and a moving length is contracted by $\gamma$:
  15 km becomes about 660 m. The ground explains the same click with a slow clock; the
  muon explains it with a short atmosphere.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **A moving clock ticks slow by γ**, because its light runs a longer path at the same $c$.
- **Every clock does it, muons included**, and no clock ever notices it about itself.
- **The frame without the slow clock sees a short ruler:** $L = L_0/\gamma$.
