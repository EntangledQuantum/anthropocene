## Name it

The dots obeyed one rule and the heights obeyed another. Charge never piles up at a junction,
and any trip round a loop climbs exactly as far as it falls:

$$\sum I_{\text{in}} = \sum I_{\text{out}} \qquad\qquad \sum_{\text{loop}} \Delta V = 0$$

Drawing heights makes the loop rule automatic, and the junction rule sets where those heights are.

<Recall id="up-ch26-recall-junction" concept="kirchhoff-junction-rule">
  <div slot="front">0.33 A flows into a bulb. How much flows out of it?</div>

  0.33 A. Current is not used up: charge that goes in comes out. What the bulb uses is energy,
  which the charge gives up as it falls through the bulb's voltage.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Recall id="up-ch26-recall-parallel" concept="equivalent-resistance">
  <div slot="front">Why does adding a bulb in parallel lower a circuit's resistance?</div>

  It adds a path. The same voltage now drives current through one more route, so more current
  flows in total, which is what lower resistance means: $1/R_\parallel = 1/R_1 + 1/R_2$.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Current splits and rejoins, and none is used up.** What arrives at a junction leaves it.
- **Potential is a height.** The battery lifts charge, the bulbs let it fall, and every loop comes back to where it started.
