## Only the rope decides

Your hand cannot change the speed. The hanger can: it sets how hard the rope is pulled tight.

## Name it

Tension straightens the rope and its mass per metre, $\mu$, resists. Their contest sets one
speed for every shape:

$$v = \sqrt{F/\mu}$$

Whatever your hand does, the rope repeats it farther along and later: $y(x,t) = f(x - vt)$.

<Recall id="up-ch15-recall-tension" concept="wave-speed-on-string">
  <div slot="front">You double the tension in a guitar string. How much faster does a pulse run along it?</div>

  About 1.41 times, which is $\sqrt 2$. Speed goes as the square root of tension, so doubling
  the speed takes four times the tension.
</Recall>

</Step>

## What to carry forward

- **The shape travels; the rope stays.** Each bead goes up and down on its own mark.
- **The rope sets the speed**, $v = \sqrt{F/\mu}$. The flick sets only the shape.
- The picture: a hump sliding along while the painted bead just rises and falls.

Next: two humps meet on one rope, and pass straight through each other.
