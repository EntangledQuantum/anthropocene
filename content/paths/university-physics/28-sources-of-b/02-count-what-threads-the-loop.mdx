## Walk round the wires

Four long wires cross the page. Walk round any closed loop, adding up the part of the field
along each step times the step's length: that total, $\oint \vec B\cdot d\vec\ell$, is the
**circulation**, and the meter reads it for whatever loop you draw.

  id="up-ch28-trace-loop"
  prompt="Draw a loop, any shape, that makes the meter read 2.51 µT·m."
  wires={[{ x: -7, y: 2, I: 3 }, { x: -3, y: -3, I: -1 }, { x: 3, y: 3, I: 4 }, { x: 7, y: -2, I: -2 }]}
  target={2}
  explanation="Wrap the 3 A and the 1 A running the other way, or the 4 A and the 2 A: either way a net 2 A threads the loop, and 2.51 µT·m is 1.26 µT·m for every ampere. The loop's size and shape never entered, and every wire outside it added exactly nothing, however close you passed."

## Name it

Round any closed loop, the circulation counts only the current threading it, walked
counterclockwise when that current comes out of the page. That is **Ampère's law**, the
circulation cousin of Gauss's law, which counted the charge inside a closed surface:

$$\oint \vec B\cdot d\vec\ell = \mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}$$

<Recall id="up-ch28-recall-ampere" concept="amperes-law">
  <div slot="front">A wire carrying 5 A passes through a square loop, well off-centre, and a second wire carrying 8 A runs just outside it. What is the circulation round the square?</div>

  $\mu_0 \times 5\ \mathrm{A} \approx 6.3\ \mu\mathrm{T\,m}$. The square's shape and the wire's
  position inside it do not matter, and the outside wire adds nothing, though it changes the
  field all round the square.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Cancel the core

An inner coil has 30 turns and carries 2 A. Around it sits a coil two and a half times as
wide and just as long, wound the other way and carrying 3 A.

The outer coil's width never entered. Slide that long side into the gap and only the outer winding threads it, which is why the field there survives. For one long coil the same rectangle gives Bℓ = μ₀(nℓ)I, so B = μ₀nI."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch28-recall-solenoid" concept="solenoid-field">
  <div slot="front">A long coil has 2000 turns per metre and carries 0.5 A. What is the field inside, and what does doubling its radius do?</div>

  $B = \mu_0 n I = 4\pi\times10^{-7} \times 2000 \times 0.5 \approx 1.3$ mT, the same all
  across the middle. Doubling the radius changes nothing: there is no radius in $\mu_0 n I$.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Circulation counts what threads the loop:** $\oint \vec B\cdot d\vec\ell = \mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}$, for any shape, with outside currents adding nothing.
- **Inside a long coil, $B = \mu_0 n I$**: uniform, along the axis, blind to the coil's width, and nearly zero outside.
