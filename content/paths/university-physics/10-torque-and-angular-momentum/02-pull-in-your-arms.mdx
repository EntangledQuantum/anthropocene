## Name it

The product that held still is the **angular momentum**. For each dumbbell it is
$\vec r\times\vec p$; for the whole spinning chair it adds up to $I\omega$. With no torque
from outside, it cannot change:

$$L = I\omega = \Big(I_{\text{you}} + \textstyle\sum m r^2\Big)\,\omega, \qquad \tau_{\text{ext}} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; I_1\omega_1 = I_2\omega_2$$

<Recall id="up-ch10-recall-diver" concept="angular-momentum">
  <div slot="front">A diver tucks mid-air and spins faster. What did she change, and what could she not?</div>

  She shrank her $I$ by pulling mass toward her spin axis. She could not change $L$: in the
  air nothing twists her about her centre of mass, so $\omega$ rises to keep $I\omega$ fixed.
</Recall>

  <div slot="front">The Sun turns once in about 25 days. Squeezed to a ball 24 km across, with nothing twisting it, how fast would it turn?</div>

  About 1500 times a second. $I$ scales as $R^2$, and $R$ shrinks by a factor of 58,000,

## What to carry forward

- **With no outside twist, $I\omega$ is fixed.** Shrink $I$ and the spin rises to pay for it.
- **Angular momentum and energy keep separate books.** Pulling in adds energy; stepping on loses it; $L$ never moves.
