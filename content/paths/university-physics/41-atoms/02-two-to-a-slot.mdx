## Name it

**Spin** is a fourth label beside n, l and m, with two values: up or down along any axis.
**Pauli exclusion** says no two electrons in an atom share all four labels, so each standing
wave (n, l, m) is a box with two places.

$$\text{subshell holds } 2(2\ell+1), \qquad \text{shell holds } 2n^2$$

<Recall id="up-ch41-recall-pauli" concept="pauli-exclusion">
  <div slot="front">Helium's two electrons fill 1s. Why can't lithium's third electron join them there, when it would have less energy?</div>

  Both 1s places are taken, and a third electron would share all four labels with one already
  there. Exclusion forbids that outright; it is not repulsion, which only costs energy. So the
  third electron goes up to 2s, farther out and loosely held.
</Recall>

## The sawtooth

Pulling one electron off neon costs 21.6 eV. Pulling one off sodium, one proton heavier, costs
5.1 eV.

<Recall id="up-ch41-recall-sodium" concept="electron-configuration">
  <div slot="front">Sodium has one more proton than neon. Why does it lose an electron for a quarter of the energy?</div>

  Neon's ten electrons fill shells 1 and 2. Sodium's eleventh must start shell 3 on its own,
  farther out and behind ten electrons that screen all but about +2 of the nucleus.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Spin gives two answers along any axis**, and exclusion allows one electron per place: two per standing wave, $2n^2$ per shell.
- **Filling from the bottom builds the periodic table.** Every row ends in a closed shell that holds tight and starts with one lone electron that lets go.

Next: bring two atoms close together, and their standing waves split into pairs.
