## Name it

A shell's catch is density times volume: the density $|\psi|^2$, times the shell's area
$4\pi r^2$, times its thickness. That product is the **radial probability**.

$$P(r)\,dr = |\psi|^2\,4\pi r^2\,dr$$

For the ground state it peaks at $a_0 = 52.9$ pm. Bohr's radius survives as the most likely
distance, not as a track.

<Recall id="up-ch41-recall-radial" concept="radial-probability">
  <div slot="front">In hydrogen's ground state |ψ|² is largest at the nucleus. Why is the most likely distance 53 pm and not zero?</div>

  The chance of a distance counts every direction at once: density times the area of the shell,
  $4\pi r^2$. Near zero the shell has no area, so it catches nothing however dense the cloud is.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Count the dark shells

The ground state has no dark shell and 2s has one. Each state is a standing wave, like a
drumhead's, and its dark places are nodes where ψ is exactly zero.

## Shape is not energy

**n** counts the nodes plus one, **l** counts the ones through the nucleus, and **m**, held at 0
here, turns the pattern. In hydrogen the energy is $E_n = -13.6\ \text{eV}/n^2$.

<Recall id="up-ch41-recall-nodes" concept="atomic-quantum-numbers">
  <div slot="front">How many dark spheres does hydrogen's 5d cloud have, and how many dark sheets through the nucleus?</div>

  Two spheres and two sheets. A 5d state has $n - 1 = 4$ nodes: $\ell = 2$ of them pass
  through the nucleus, and $n - \ell - 1 = 2$ are spheres.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **The atom is a cloud, and a chance of distance is density times shell area.** Dense is not the same as likely.
- **n, l and m label a 3D standing wave**: n − l − 1 dark spheres, l dark sheets, and in hydrogen an energy set by n alone.
