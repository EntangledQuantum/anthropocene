## Name it

Every contact in that scene was one interaction, read from each end:

$$\vec F_{\text{A on B}} = -\,\vec F_{\text{B on A}}$$

Same size, opposite directions, on **two different bodies**. That last part is why a pair
never cancels: its two arrows land in two different sums.

<Recall id="up-ch04-recall-weight-partner" concept="third-law-pair">
  <div slot="front">What is the third-law partner of your weight, and what does it act on?</div>

  Your pull on the Earth, upward, acting on the planet. It is not the floor's push on you.
  That is a different interaction, and it vanishes when you jump.
</Recall>

</Step>

## What to carry forward

- **One interaction, two arrows, two bodies.** Equal, opposite, never in the same sum, and no
  mass condition anywhere.
- **A contact force is whatever the motion demands.** A scale reads its own push, not your weight.
- **The boundary is your move.** Pairs inside it cancel, so draw it where the force you want is
  the only one crossing.

Next chapter keeps these laws and changes the costumes: ramps, ropes, friction and circles.
