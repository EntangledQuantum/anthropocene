<Step>

## Name it

The ramp was your force. The flat line was no force. That is all Newton's second law says:

$$\vec F_{\text{net}} = m\vec a$$

Force sets how fast the velocity **changes**. It says nothing about what the velocity **is**.

<Recall id="up-ch4-recall-inertia" concept="inertia">
  <div slot="front">A spacecraft coasts between stars with its engines off. What is keeping it moving?</div>

  Nothing. Constant velocity is the free state and costs nothing to keep. Only a *change* in
  velocity needs a net force.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Recall id="up-ch4-recall-fbd" concept="free-body-diagram">
  <div slot="front">Before you add an arrow to a free-body diagram, what must you be able to say?</div>

  What exerts it. Every force is one body acting on another: the Earth, the floor, a rope,

## What to carry forward

- **Velocity persists. Change costs.** Net force is the price of a change in velocity.
- **Forces add as arrows.** Zero net force is a closed loop of arrows that are all still there.
