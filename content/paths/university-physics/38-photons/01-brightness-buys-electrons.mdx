<Step>

## Name it

Light is absorbed in lumps, **photons**, each carrying $E = hf$. An electron takes one whole
photon, pays the metal's **work function** $\phi$ to get out, and keeps the rest:

$$K_{\max} = hf - \phi$$

Sodium's $\phi$ is 2.36 eV, so its cutoff is green light,
525 nm: the yellow glow of a sodium street lamp cannot free a single electron from sodium.

<Recall id="up-ch38-recall-photon" concept="photon">
  <div slot="front">A lamp is turned up to twice the brightness, same colour. What happens to each photon's energy, and to the number of photons?</div>

  Each photon's energy stays $hf$, set by the colour. Twice as many photons arrive each
  second, so twice as many electrons leave, with the same top speed.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Step>

## Stop them with a voltage

A reverse voltage turns back every electron with less than $eV$ of kinetic energy. The
voltage that just stops the current measures the fastest one.

<StopThePhotocurrent
  id="up-ch38-measure-h"
  prompt="For three colours, raise the voltage until the current just stops."
  explanation="Your slope times e is {h} × 10⁻³⁴ J·s: Planck's constant, 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴, from a lamp and a voltmeter. The points lie on eV₀ = hf − φ. The green and yellow lines give no point at all: they sit below sodium's cutoff, where the line meets zero."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch38-recall-stopping" concept="stopping-potential">
  <div slot="front">You repeat the measurement with a cesium plate (φ = 2.14 eV) instead of sodium (2.36 eV). How does the line change?</div>

  Same slope, $h/e$: that belongs to light, not to the metal. The line moves up by 0.22 V
  and meets zero at a lower frequency, cesium's cutoff.
</Recall>

</Step>

## The wave's clock

Suppose light were a wave spreading its energy evenly over the plate. Dim light brings
1 mW to each square metre, and one sodium atom's patch is about 0.3 nm across.

## What to carry forward

- **Light is absorbed in lumps of $hf$.** Colour sets the size of each lump; brightness sets
  how many.
- **One photon frees one electron, which keeps $hf - \phi$.** Below the cutoff no brightness helps.
- **Stopping voltage against frequency is a line of slope $h/e$** for every metal.
