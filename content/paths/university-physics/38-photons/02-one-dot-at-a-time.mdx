## Name it

Light is detected in whole photons, each at one spot, each carrying $hf$. Where one lands is
random, and the odds follow the wave's intensity, dark stripes included:

$$P(\text{lands near } y) \propto I(y)$$

<Recall id="up-ch38-recall-duality" concept="wave-particle-duality">
  <div slot="front">Photons go through two slits one at a time. What does a single photon leave on the screen, and what makes the stripes?</div>

  One dot, at one spot. The stripes are the odds: the wave through both slits sets where
  photons are likely to land, and the pattern appears only as dots pile up.
</Recall>

</Step>

## A photon hits back

X-rays of 71.1 pm strike a block of carbon, and those that come out at 90° are 2.4 pm longer,
whatever the block is made of. Treat each X-ray as a photon of momentum $h/\lambda$ hitting one
free electron.

/>

<Recall id="up-ch38-recall-compton" concept="photon-momentum">
  <div slot="front">Why do scattered X-rays come out with a longer wavelength?</div>

  The photon carries momentum $h/\lambda$ and kicks the electron, which leaves with kinetic
  energy. That energy comes out of the photon, so $hc/\lambda$ drops and $\lambda$ grows, by
  $\frac{h}{mc}(1 - \cos\theta)$.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Light arrives in whole photons, each at one spot.** The wave sets the odds of where.
- **Dark stripes need both slits.** Cover one and photons land where none did.
- **A photon carries momentum $h/\lambda$** and bounces off an electron like a particle.
