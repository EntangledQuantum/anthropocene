## Name it

A source moving toward you at $v_S$ shortens every wavelength in the air to
$(v - v_S)/f$. A listener moving toward the source at $v_L$ meets those crests faster. Only
the speed along the line between them counts, and the distance appears nowhere:

$$f' = f\,\frac{v + v_L}{v - v_S}$$

<Recall id="up-ch16-recall-steady-pitch" concept="doppler-effect">
  <div slot="front">An ambulance drives straight toward you at a steady speed. Why does its siren not rise in pitch as it gets closer?</div>

  The pitch is set by how bunched the crests are, and at a steady speed every crest is
  bunched by the same amount. Getting closer makes it louder, not higher. The pitch falls
  only as it passes, when its velocity stops pointing at you.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Stop the throb

A 1% change in pitch is hard to hear on its own. Two notes 1% apart are easy: together they
swell and fade.

<KillTheThrob
  id="up-ch16-stop-throb"
  prompt="Slide the rider along your fork's tine until the throb stops, then check."
  explanation="About two-thirds of the way up, where your fork drops to 440 Hz. Two tones drift in and out of step once for every hertz between them, so the sound swells |f₁ − f₂| times a second. Slide on past the match and the throb returns: the beat rate says how far apart two notes are, not which is higher."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch16-recall-beats" concept="beats">
  <div slot="front">A piano string and a 440 Hz fork sound together and throb 3 times a second. What is the string's frequency, and how do you find out which?</div>

  437 Hz or 443 Hz. Tighten the string slightly: if the throb speeds up, it was already
  sharp at 443; if it slows, it was flat at 437.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **A moving source bunches its crests ahead and stretches them behind.** The pitch is shifted and steady; only the velocity along the line to you counts.
- **The air is the stage.** Moving the whistle and moving the listener are different shifts.
- **Two close tones throb at their difference**, which is how a small shift becomes easy to hear.
