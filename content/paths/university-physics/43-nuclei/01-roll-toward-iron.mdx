## Name it

The mass a nucleus is missing, times $c^2$, is its binding energy:

$$B = \left(Z m_\text{H} + N m_n - M\right)c^2$$

$B/A$ is the depth of the valley you dragged the nucleus along. A reaction releases $Q = \Delta m\,c^2$, exactly what its nucleons gain by settling deeper.

<Recall id="up-ch43-recall-fission-pays" concept="nuclear-binding-energy">
  <div slot="front">Pulling a nucleus apart always costs energy. So why does splitting uranium release it?</div>

  The split does not stop at loose nucleons. The two fragments hold each nucleon about 0.8 MeV
  more tightly than uranium did, so 236 nucleons each settle 0.8 MeV deeper: nearly 200 MeV.
</Recall>

</Step>

## What to carry forward

- **Binding is missing mass.** A nucleus weighs less than its parts by $B/c^2$, and a reaction releases whatever mass its products lose.
- **Everything rolls toward iron.** Light nuclei pay by fusing, heavy ones by splitting, and iron, on the floor, pays neither way.

Next: heavy nuclei do not wait to be split. They decay on their own, at random, and the randomness keeps perfect time.
