<Step>

## Name it

Each atomic level becomes $N$ levels, spread over a band whose width is set by how strongly
neighbours share an electron, $t$:

$$E_j = \varepsilon_0 - 2t\cos\frac{j\pi}{N+1}, \qquad j = 1, \dots, N$$

A pair splits by $2t$, and no row, however long, gets wider than $4t$.

<Recall id="up-ch42-recall-bands" concept="energy-bands">
  <div slot="front">Two atoms split a level by 1 eV. Roughly how wide is the band a million of them make?</div>

  Just under 2 eV. The pair's split is $2t$ and a long row's band is $4t$. A million atoms add a
  million levels, all packed inside.
</Recall>

</Step>

## A full band carries nothing

Each state in a band is a way of moving through the crystal, and it holds two electrons. A push
from a battery can only move electrons into states that are empty.

<PourTheSea
  id="up-ch42-pour-the-sea"
  prompt="Pour in electrons until a push drives the most current."
  explanation="About one electron per atom: a half-full band, like copper's. The electrons were moving all along, as many left as right; the push tips the balance by moving the top ones into empty states. At two per atom every state is taken, the push shifts the sea onto itself, and nothing flows. That is an insulator, and it is how silicon fills its lower band."

<Step>

## The gap decides

Silicon and diamond are both a full band under an empty one. Only the gap differs: 1.1 eV in
silicon, 5.5 eV in diamond. Heat lifts electrons across it in numbers that go as
$e^{-E_g/2kT}$, and at room temperature $kT$ is 0.025 eV.

<Recall id="up-ch42-recall-gap" concept="band-gap">
  <div slot="front">Warm silicon from 20 °C to 100 °C and its current rises about a hundredfold. Warm copper and it falls. Why the opposite answers?</div>

  Copper's band is half full, so its carriers are all there already; heat only shakes the lattice
  and scatters them. Silicon's carriers must be lifted across a gap, in numbers that grow as
  $e^{-E_g/2kT}$, and that swamps the extra scattering.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Atoms together make bands.** Each level becomes as many levels as there are atoms, packed into a band about $4t$ wide.
- **A push needs empty states.** A part-full band conducts; a full one carries nothing, however many electrons it holds.
- **The gap sets the leak.** Heat frees electrons as $e^{-E_g/2kT}$: a few in silicon, none in diamond.
