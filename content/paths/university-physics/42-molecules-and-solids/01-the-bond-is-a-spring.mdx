## Name it

The stiffness is the curvature at the bottom of the well, and the mass that rings is the reduced mass:

$$k = \left.\frac{d^2U}{dr^2}\right|_{r_e}, \qquad \omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}}, \qquad \mu = \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$$

For H–Cl, $\mu$ is 0.98 u, less than the hydrogen alone, because the hydrogen does nearly all the moving.

<Recall id="up-ch42-recall-vibration" concept="molecular-vibration">
  <div slot="front">What sets how fast a bond vibrates: the depth of its well or the shape of its bottom?</div>

  The shape of the bottom. Its curvature is the spring constant, and $\omega = \sqrt{k/\mu}$. The depth
  only says how much energy breaks the bond.
</Recall>

<Recall id="up-ch42-recall-reduced-mass" concept="reduced-mass">
  <div slot="front">Why does swapping ³⁵Cl for ³⁷Cl barely change H–Cl's vibration, when swapping H for D changes it by more than a quarter?</div>

  The frequency depends on $\mu = m_1 m_2/(m_1 + m_2)$, which is close to the lighter mass. The
  hydrogen does nearly all the moving, so only its mass counts.

## What to carry forward

- **Near the bottom, every bond is a spring.** Its stiffness is the well's curvature there, and $\omega = \sqrt{k/\mu}$.
- **The light atom sets the pace.** Doubling it moves the infrared line by about $1/\sqrt2$; changing the heavy one hardly moves it.
