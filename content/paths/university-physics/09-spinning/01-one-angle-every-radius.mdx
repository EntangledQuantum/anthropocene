## Name it

The stripe turns through one angle $\theta$ and everything on it goes along. In radians, a
point at radius $r$ runs an arc $s = r\theta$, so it moves at

$$v = \omega r, \qquad \omega = \frac{d\theta}{dt}$$

$\omega$ is the **angular velocity**, shared by every point of a rigid body: 45 rpm is
4.71 rad/s, and 4.71 × 0.125 m is bug B's 0.59 m/s.

<Recall id="up-ch09-recall-omega" concept="angular-velocity">
  <div slot="front">Bug B sits three times as far from a turntable's centre as bug A. Compare their angular velocities, then their speeds.</div>

  The same $\omega$: they turn through the same angle every second. B moves three times as
  fast, because $v = \omega r$. $\omega$ counts radians per second; only $v$ is in metres per
  second.
</Recall>

</Step>

## The road is a belt that stands still

A tyre is belted to the road, and the road never moves: the contact point is at rest, so
the axle travels $v = \omega R$. The pebble's velocity is two arrows added: forward with the
axle at 5 m/s, and round the axle at $\omega R$, also 5 m/s.

<Recall id="up-ch09-recall-rolling" concept="rolling-without-slipping">
  <div slot="front">A car drives at 30 m/s. How fast is the bottom of a tyre moving over the road? The top?</div>

  The bottom is at rest: rolling without slipping means the contact point moves with the
  road. The top moves at 60 m/s. At every instant the wheel pivots about its contact point.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **One angle, every radius.** A rigid body has one $\omega$; each point moves at $v = \omega r$.
- **Rolling is a constraint.** The contact point is at rest, the axle moves at $\omega R$, and the top at twice that.
- The picture: a pebble's row of arches, with a sharp cusp each time it touches the road.
