## Name it

Each piece of a spinning body moves at its own $v = \omega r$ and carries
$\tfrac12 m \omega^2 r^2$. Add the pieces and $\omega$ comes out in front:

$$K_{\text{rot}} = \tfrac12\Big(\sum m r^2\Big)\,\omega^2 = \tfrac12 I\omega^2$$

$I = \sum m r^2$ is the **moment of inertia**: $mR^2$ for a hoop, $\tfrac12 mR^2$ for a disc,
whose mass sits closer in.

<Recall id="up-ch09-recall-inertia" concept="moment-of-inertia">
  <div slot="front">You slide a bead from 6 cm to 12 cm along a spinning bar. By what factor does its share of the bar's rotational inertia change?</div>

  Four times. Each piece counts as $m r^2$: twice the distance means twice the speed at the
  same $\omega$, and energy goes as speed squared. The mass on the bar is unchanged.
</Recall>

<Recall id="up-ch09-recall-friction" concept="rotational-kinetic-energy">
  <div slot="front">A disc rolls down a ramp without slipping, with friction from the ramp acting on it the whole way. How much of m g h does friction take?</div>

  None. Static friction pushes on the contact point, which is momentarily at rest, so it does
  no work. It only steers energy into the spin: all of $mgh$ arrives at the bottom, split
  between $\tfrac12 mv^2$ and $\tfrac12 I\omega^2$.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Rotational inertia is where the mass sits.** $I = \sum m r^2$: each piece counts by the square of its distance.
- **Spinning stores $\tfrac12 I\omega^2$.** A rolling body pays for its spin out of the same $mgh$, so the larger $I/mR^2$, the slower it rolls.
- The picture: the same two beads at the hub and near the ends, and the weight falling fast, then slowly.
