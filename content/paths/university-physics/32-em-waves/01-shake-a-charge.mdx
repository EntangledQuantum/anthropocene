## Name it

The kink is a change in $\vec E$ running through space. A changing $\vec E$ makes a $\vec B$ and a changing $\vec B$ makes an $\vec E$, and Maxwell found the pair carries itself along at one speed, built from the constants of Coulomb's and Ampère's laws:

$$c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}} = 2.998\times10^{8}\ \text{m/s}$$

Measure $\varepsilon_0 = 8.854\times10^{-12}$ F/m with a capacitor and $\mu_0 = 1.257\times10^{-6}$ N/A² with a coil, and the speed of light falls out.

<Recall id="up-ch32-recall-c" concept="em-wave-speed">
  <div slot="front">A radio mast and a lightbulb are both switched on 3 km away. Whose signal reaches you first?</div>

  Neither. Both are the same kind of field wave, and in vacuum every one of them moves at
  $c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}$, 10 µs for 3 km. The source sets the shape of the
  wave, never its speed.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Freeze it

Far from a charge shaken steadily, the kinks line up into a wave: $\vec E$ up and down the page, $\vec B$ in and out of it. Here $\vec E$ is frozen, and $\vec B$'s pattern is yours to slide.

<Recall id="up-ch32-recall-in-phase" concept="em-wave">
  <div slot="front">Freeze a light wave travelling east. Where along it is B biggest, and which way does it point?</div>

  Exactly where E is biggest. B is at right angles to E and to east, turned so that E × B

  running both ways.

## What to carry forward

- **A change in the field is news, and news travels at c.** Far away, the field still points at where the charge was.
- **c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀)** belongs to empty space. No source can hurry it.
- The picture: **two transverse arrows, E and B, skating forward together**, with E × B pointing the way they go.

Next: the wave carries energy and momentum, enough to push a sail.
