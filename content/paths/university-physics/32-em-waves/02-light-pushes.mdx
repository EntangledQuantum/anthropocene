## Name it

A wave of peak field $E_0$ brings energy at $I = \tfrac12 c\varepsilon_0 E_0^2$ through each square metre each second, and with every joule it brings momentum $1/c$. Stopping it takes that momentum; reflecting it takes twice as much:

$$P_\text{absorb} = \frac{I}{c}, \qquad P_\text{reflect} = \frac{2I}{c}$$

Sunlight's 1361 W/m² is a field swinging to about 1000 V/m, and it presses on a mirror with 9 µPa, a ten-billionth of air pressure that never stops.

<Recall id="up-ch32-recall-mirror" concept="radiation-pressure">
  <div slot="front">A laser beam is aimed at a black card and then at a mirror. Which is pushed harder, and by how much?</div>

  The mirror, twice as hard. The black card stops the light and takes its momentum $U/c$
  once, a pressure $I/c$. The mirror sends it straight back, so the light's momentum is
  reversed, and the card takes $2U/c$.
</Recall>

## Measure c with chocolate

Take the turntable out of a microwave oven, heat a bar of chocolate for twenty seconds, and it melts in spots. The label on the back says 2.45 GHz.

<PinTheWavelength
  id="up-ch32-chocolate-c"
  prompt="Put the pins exactly one wavelength apart."
  explanation="12.2 cm, two spots apart. The wave bounces off the metal wall into a standing wave, and heating goes as E², so the up-loop and the down-loop both melt. Spots come every half wavelength, and 0.122 m × 2.45 × 10⁹ Hz = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch32-recall-oven" concept="em-spectrum">
  <div slot="front">Neighbouring melted spots in a 2.45 GHz oven are 6.1 cm apart. What is the wavelength, and what speed does it give?</div>

  12.2 cm. Heating goes as $E^2$, so both halves of each wavelength melt: spots come every
  $\lambda/2$. Then $c = \lambda f = 0.122 \times 2.45\times10^9 = 3.0\times10^8$ m/s.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Light carries momentum U/c.** Stopped, it pushes with I/c; turned straight round, with 2I/c.
- **One wave at every wavelength.** Radio to gamma rays is the same field, going at c, with λf = c.
- **A standing EM wave has hot spots every λ/2**, because the energy goes as E².
