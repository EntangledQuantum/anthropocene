## Cancel the arrows

On the right, each part's voltage is an arrow, drawn with the current pointing right. The
coil's always points straight up, the capacitor's straight down, and together with the lamp's
they must close on the supply's 10 V.

## Name it

Each part's opposition is its **reactance**, $X_L = \omega L$ rising with frequency and
$X_C = 1/\omega C$ falling; adding the arrows gives the **impedance**. Where the two
reactances cancel is resonance, and it is chapter 14's driven oscillator in wires: charge for
position, $L$ for mass, $1/C$ for the spring, $R$ for damping.

$$Z = R + i\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right),\qquad \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}},\qquad V_C = Q\,V,\quad Q = \frac{1}{R}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} = 5$$

<Recall id="up-ch31-recall-resonance" concept="series-rlc-resonance">
  <div slot="front">You swap the tuned loop's 80 Ω lamp for a 40 Ω one. At resonance, what happens to the capacitor's voltage?</div>

  It doubles, to 100 V. The resonant frequency does not move, since it depends only on $L$
  and $C$, but the current doubles to $V/R$ and so does $Q$. Less damping, a taller peak,
  exactly as for the spring in chapter 14.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Tune the radio

An AM radio's tuner is this loop, with the antenna as the supply and every station driving it
at once. You turn a capacitor rather than the frequency, and the station at
$1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})$ is the one whose arrows cancel.

<Recall id="up-ch31-recall-reactance" concept="reactance">
  <div slot="front">You double the frequency across a coil, then across a capacitor. What happens to each one's reactance?</div>

  The coil's doubles, $X_L = \omega L$: a faster change in current takes a bigger push. The
  capacitor's halves, $X_C = 1/\omega C$: at a higher frequency the plates are refilled more
  often, so more current flows for the same voltage.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Series voltages add as arrows.** The coil's and capacitor's point opposite ways.
- **Resonance is where they cancel**, at $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}$: current biggest and in
  step, and each of the two can carry $Q$ times the supply.
- **Same oscillator, new costume**: a mass on a spring, a swing, a tuned radio.
