<Step>

## Name it

Your last column is the **thin-lens equation**. The three coloured rays find the same point
without it: in level and out through F, straight through the centre, in through F and out level.

$$\frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{f} \qquad\qquad m = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}$$

The minus sign in $m$ is the flip you saw: at 15 cm the image was upside down and twice the
candle’s height, $-30/15$.

<Recall id="up-ch34-recall-lens-equation" concept="thin-lens-equation">
  <div slot="front">A projector’s lens has f = 10 cm. You move the slide from 12 cm to 11 cm from the lens. Does the screen go nearer or further, and roughly how far?</div>

  Much further. At 12 cm the image forms at 60 cm; at 11 cm, at 110 cm. As the slide nears
  the focal point the image runs away: $1/d_i = 1/f - 1/d_o$.
</Recall>

</Step>

  <div slot="front">What makes an image real, and why does blocking part of the lens not cut a piece out of it?</div>

  The light really meets there, so a screen shows it. Every part of the lens carries light

## What to carry forward

- **A converging lens brings each point’s rays back to one point**, at $1/d_o + 1/d_i = 1/f$. The closer the object, the further the image.
- **Outside f the image is real and upside down; inside f it is virtual and upright.** Three rays locate either one without the formula.

Next: stack two lenses, and the first one’s image becomes the second one’s object.
