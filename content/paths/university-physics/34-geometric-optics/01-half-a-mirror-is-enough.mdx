## Name it

Every dashed line ran on to a person behind the glass: your **image**, as far behind the
mirror as you are in front, the same size and the same way up. The light from your toes and
your head crosses the glass halfway to that image, so the glass need only be half as tall as you.

$$d_\text{image} = d_\text{object} \qquad\qquad \text{least mirror} = \tfrac12\,\text{your height}$$

<Recall id="up-ch34-recall-half-mirror" concept="plane-mirror-image">
  <div slot="front">A shop fits a 60 cm mirror so a 1.70 m customer sees her whole outfit. Is that enough, and does it help to step back?</div>

  No, and no. She needs 85 cm, half her height, with its bottom edge halfway up to her eye.
  Stepping back changes nothing: her image steps back with her.
</Recall>

<Recall id="up-ch34-recall-virtual" concept="virtual-image">
  <div slot="front">What makes an image virtual, and could a screen show it?</div>

  The light only seems to come from it: the rays reaching your eye, traced straight back,
  cross there, but no light ever does. A screen there shows nothing. Your eye and a camera
  can still use it.

## What to carry forward

- **A flat mirror puts your image as far behind the glass as you are in front.** It is virtual: light seems to come from it and never reaches it.
- **Half your height of glass shows all of you**, at any distance.

Next: a lens bends light to a point where a screen *can* catch it.
