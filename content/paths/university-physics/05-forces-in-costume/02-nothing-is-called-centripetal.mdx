## Slow it down

The slower you swing, the less the bottom has to push. Somewhere it runs out.

<Step>

## Name it

At the top both forces point at the centre, and together they supply the turn:

$$mg + N = \frac{mv^2}{R}$$

The bucket can push but never pull, so $N \ge 0$ and $v \ge \sqrt{gR}$ whatever the mass of
water. No arrow was called centripetal: the word names the job, and here gravity and the
bucket share it.

<Recall id="up-ch05-recall-top" concept="vertical-circle">
  <div slot="front">A roller-coaster car runs upside down over the top of a loop of radius 10 m. What is the slowest speed it can have there?</div>

  About 9.9 m/s, from v² = gR. Gravity and the track both point at the centre, and the track
  can only push, so gravity alone sets the floor.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **No force is called centripetal.** List the real forces; the inward part of their sum is mv²/R.
- **Contact forces push and never pull.** That one inequality sets the slowest speed at the top of a loop.
- **Friction fills the gap**, in whichever direction the budget needs.

Next: the same forces, added up along a path instead of at an instant. That sum is work.
