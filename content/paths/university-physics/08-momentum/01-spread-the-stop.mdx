## Name it

The shaded area is the **impulse**. It always equals the change in **momentum**, mass times
velocity:

$$\vec p = m\vec v, \qquad \vec J = \int \vec F\,dt = \Delta\vec p$$

That is Newton's second law, $\vec F = d\vec p/dt$, added up over the whole push. The
momentum fixes the area; the hand chooses the shape.

<Recall id="up-ch08-recall-knees" concept="impulse">
  <div slot="front">You land from a jump with the same momentum whether or not you bend your knees. Why does bending protect them?</div>

  The ground has to remove the same momentum either way, so the area under force against
  time is fixed. Bending stretches the time, and a fixed area over a longer time is a
  lower peak. Joints fail on the peak.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Recall id="up-ch08-recall-reversal" concept="momentum">
  <div slot="front">A 0.145 kg ball arrives at 35 m/s and leaves at 45 m/s the other way. Why is its change in momentum not 0.145 × 10?</div>

  Momentum is an arrow. Going from 35 one way to 45 the other is a change of 80 m/s, so
  $\Delta p = 0.145 \times 80 = 11.6$ kg·m/s. Reversing a motion costs the arrival and the
  departure together.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **The momentum sets the area. You set the shape.** A longer stop is a lower one.
- **Momentum is an arrow.** Turning a motion round costs the arrival and the departure together.
