## Name it

During the crunch the bumper pushes A and B equally hard, in opposite directions, for the
same time. Their impulses cancel, so nothing inside the system can change the total:

$$m_A v_A + m_B v_B = m_A v_A' + m_B v_B'$$

Kinetic energy has no such protection. How much survives depends on the bumper.

<Recall id="up-ch08-recall-skaters" concept="momentum-conservation">
  <div slot="front">Two skaters at rest push off each other. What is their total momentum afterwards, and where did their kinetic energy come from?</div>

  Zero, as before: each push on one skater is matched by an equal and opposite push on the
  other for the same time. Their kinetic energy came from their muscles. Momentum is
  conserved inside the system; kinetic energy is not.
</Recall>

## Light into heavy

Velcro keeps none of the closing speed and a perfect spring returns all of it. In between,
the carts separate at $e$ times the speed they closed at, and the kinetic energy lost is
$\tfrac12(1-e^2)\,\mu\,(v_A-v_B)^2$ with $\mu = m_Am_B/(m_A+m_B)$.

## What to carry forward

- **The total momentum never moves** inside the boundary. Every internal push has an equal and opposite partner.
- **Kinetic energy is the part that depends on the bumper.** $e$ decides how much comes back.
- The picture: two momentum arrows that still reach the same white mark after the flash.
