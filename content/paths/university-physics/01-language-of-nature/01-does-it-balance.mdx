On 23 September 1999 the Mars Climate Orbiter fired its engine to slip into orbit and was
never heard from again. It came in roughly 170 km lower than planned and broke up.

Nobody had done the arithmetic wrong. The ground software that tracked the small thruster
firings reported each impulse in **pound-force seconds**. The navigation software that
consumed the file read those numbers as **newton seconds**. Both files were full of
impulses. Both teams were right about what kind of quantity they were handling.

Here is the part worth sitting with. The two quantities are the *same kind of thing*.
Any check that asks "is this an impulse?" passes the file every single time. So there are
two different questions to ask about every number in physics, and this lesson builds both:

- **What kind of thing is it?** That is its dimension, and it can reject a formula you have
  never evaluated.
- **How much of it is there?** That is its unit, and no amount of dimensional care will
  catch a mistake there.

## A formula you can reject on sight

A pendulum of length $L$ swings under gravity $g$. Its period is a time.

That is not bookkeeping. That is a **prediction**: the period of a pendulum cannot depend

experiments to become confident of it. You needed three tiles.

## The structure underneath

Seven base dimensions — mass, length, time, electric current, temperature, amount of
substance, luminous intensity — and every physical quantity is a point in that
seven-dimensional space. A force sits at $\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}$. An
energy sits at $\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}^2\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}$.

Multiplying quantities **adds** their exponent vectors. Raising to a power **scales** one.
And an equation can only be true if its two sides are the same point, because there is no
sense in which a force could equal an energy.

That is the whole method, and everything else is reading the ledger.

<Tier level="advanced" title="what the 2019 redefinition of the SI actually did">

Since 20 May 2019 not one SI unit is defined by an object or an experiment. Seven
constants were assigned exact numerical values, and the units are whatever makes those
numbers come out: the caesium hyperfine frequency at 9 192 631 770 Hz fixes the second,
the speed of light at 299 792 458 m/s fixes the metre, and the Planck constant at exactly
$6.62607015\times10^{-34}$ J·s fixes the kilogram.

That last one only works because of the structure above. The Planck constant has dimension
$\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}^2\,\mathsf{T}^{-1}$ — it *carries a mass*. Fix $h$, and you have
already fixed the metre and the second, and the kilogram has nowhere left to hide. The
cylinder in Sèvres was retired not because it was badly made but because a point in
exponent space is a better anchor than a lump of platinum that loses atoms.

One nice consequence: the elementary charge is now exact, so 1 eV is exactly
$1.602176634\times10^{-19}$ J. It used to be a measured quantity with an uncertainty.

</Tier>

## Reading the mismatch

A mismatch is not a red cross. It is a message telling you what is missing.

Below, the left side is the drag force on a body moving through air, and the right side is
someone's memory of the formula: a density, a frontal area, a speed. It does not balance.
The ledger names the gap.

Read what happened. The diagnostic did not say *wrong*; it said **short by a velocity**,
and there was only one honest way to supply one. Drag really does go as $v^2$, and the
ledger got you there without a wind tunnel.

## What it will not do for you

The method is powerful in a narrow way, and knowing the edge of it is worth as much as
knowing the method.

<Recall id="up-ch1-recall-reject" concept="dimensional-analysis">
  <div slot="front">You are handed a formula you have never seen, with no numbers in it. Name one thing you can establish about it immediately, and one thing you can never establish this way.</div>

  You can establish whether both sides are the **same kind of quantity** — whether they sit
  at the same point in the space of base-dimension exponents. If they do not, the formula is
  wrong and no experiment is needed.

  You can never establish any **dimensionless** part of it: a factor of 2π, a ½, a sign, or
  whether the numbers on each side are in compatible units. Those are invisible to the
  ledger, and the Mars Climate Orbiter was lost inside that blind spot.

## The other half: multiplying by one

So: 55 miles per hour. What is that in metres per second?

The move is not to remember a rule. It is to multiply by 1, repeatedly, in a form that
does some work. The statement "1 mi = 1609.344 m" is an identity between two names for the
same length, so applying it changes nothing physical — it swaps a symbol and rescales the
number in one act.

<UnitChain
  start={{ value: 55, units: { mi: 1, h: -1 } }}
  identities={['mi>m', 'h>s', 'ft>m', 'min>s']}
  target={{ m: 1, s: -1 }}
  caption="Apply identities until the mile and the hour are struck out and only metres and seconds are left. Watch the dimension readout while you do it: it never moves, because every factor you applied was a 1. There are two decoy identities in the rack — using them is not wrong, only pointless."
/>

Two things to notice while you work.

The units **cancel**, and the cancellation is the bookkeeping that tells you which way round
to apply each identity. There is no direction to memorise: if the mile is upstairs, you need
the identity that puts a mile downstairs, and if you apply it the wrong way the symbol
doubles instead of vanishing and you can see it.

The **dimension never moves**. However long the chain gets, the quantity is still a speed,
because every factor was a 1. That invariance is the entire licence to convert at all.

<Recall id="up-ch1-recall-chain" concept="unit-conversion">
  <div slot="front">Converting 90 km/h to m/s, you write the factor for hours-to-seconds. Which way up does it go, and how do you know without remembering a rule?</div>

  Seconds on top, hours on the bottom — and you know because the hour arrives in the
  *denominator* of km/h, so you need an hour in the *numerator* of your factor to cancel it.
  The factor is 1 h / 3600 s, applied so that the h cancels, giving 90 × 1000 / 3600 = 25 m/s.

  The cancellation decides the direction. If you get it backwards the units come out as
  km·h⁻²·s or some other unreadable thing, which is the error message telling you so.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- A physical quantity is a **number, a unit, and a point in exponent space**. The third one
  is checkable on paper, and checking it is the cheapest test in physics.
- The method is **predictive, not merely defensive**: with only a length and a gravitational
  field on the table, there is exactly one time you can build, so the pendulum period cannot
  depend on the bob.
- It is **blind to every pure number** — factors, signs, and the unit you happened to write
  a correct dimension in. Both checks, every time.
