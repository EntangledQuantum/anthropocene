## Build a time

A pendulum sets its clock with only a string length $L$ and gravity $g$. A period is a time,
so whatever you build from them has to weigh the same as $T$ on every beam.

<ThreeBeamScale
  id="up-ch01-build-a-time"
  prompt="Set the powers of L and g until all three beams are level."
  left={[{ key: 'period', exponent: 1 }]}
  right={[{ key: 'length', exponent: 1, free: true }, { key: 'gravity', exponent: 1, free: true }]}
  explanation="L to the ½ times g to the −½, which is √(L/g). Only g carries time, as T⁻², so only the power −½ makes one T; the L that g brings along then needs L to the ½ to cancel it. No beam ever asked for mass. Nothing else on the table carries M, so any power of the bob's mass would tip the first beam, which is why the two bobs kept step."
/>

</Step>

<Step>

## Name it

On the beams, $g$ is $\mathsf{L}\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}$ and a force is
$\mathsf{M}\,\mathsf{L}\,\mathsf{T}^{-2}$; multiplying quantities adds their exponents. An
equation can be true only when every beam is level.

$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$$

The $2\pi$ comes from solving the motion, and no beam can see it.

<Recall id="up-ch01-recall-beams" concept="dimensional-analysis">
  <div slot="front">You are handed a formula with no numbers in it. What can you prove about it on the spot, and what can you never prove this way?</div>

  You can prove whether both sides are the same kind of quantity: every beam level. You can
  never see a pure number such as $2\pi$ or ½, and never whether the units within one
  dimension agree.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Multiply by one

1000 m and 1 km are the same length, so 1000 m over 1 km is exactly 1. Multiplying by 1
cannot change a speed, only what it is written in.

<FlipToCancel
  id="up-ch01-flip-to-cancel"
  prompt="Turn the cards over until 90 km/h is written in m/s."
  start={{ value: 90, units: { km: 1, h: -1 } }}
  cards={[
    { top: { n: 1000, unit: 'm' }, bottom: { n: 1, unit: 'km' } },
    { top: { n: 1, unit: 'h' }, bottom: { n: 3600, unit: 's' } },
  ]}
  flipped={[true, true]}
  target={{ m: 1, s: -1 }}
  explanation="90 × 1000 ÷ 3600 = 25 m/s, the speed you found by driving. You never needed to remember whether to multiply or divide: the km on top needed a km underneath to cancel, and the h underneath needed an h on top. The arrow never moved, because every card was worth exactly 1."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch01-recall-cancel" concept="unit-conversion">
  <div slot="front">Turning 25 m/s into km/h, which way up goes the card relating hours and seconds, and how do you know without a rule?</div>

  3600 s over 1 h. The s sits underneath in m/s, so the card needs s on top to cancel it.
  Put it the other way and the result reads s², which tells you so.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- Every quantity sits on the beams. An equation that tips is wrong before you evaluate it,
  and a level one can tell you what a result cannot depend on.
- The beams are blind to pure numbers and to units within one dimension.
- Converting is multiplying by one: the cancellation picks the direction, and the quantity
  never changes.

about them.
