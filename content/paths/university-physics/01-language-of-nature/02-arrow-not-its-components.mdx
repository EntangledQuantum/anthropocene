<Step>

## Name it

$\hat e_1$ and $\hat e_2$ are two perpendicular arrows of length one, and you placed them. The
components say how far to go along each to reach the tip:

$$\vec A = A_1\,\hat e_1 + A_2\,\hat e_2, \qquad |\vec A| = \sqrt{A_1^2 + A_2^2}$$

Turn the basis and both numbers change, and the two legs still land on the same tip.

<Recall id="up-ch01-recall-one-arrow" concept="vector-components">
  <div slot="front">A velocity reads (4, 3) m/s on one grid and (5, 0) m/s on another. How many velocities is that, and how can you tell?</div>

  One. Both have length 5 m/s: the second grid is the first turned 36.9° so that $\hat e_1$
  lies along the motion. To tell whether two descriptions are one arrow, compare something
  that mentions no axis, such as the length.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Step>

## How much of this lies along that

What the rail kept is a dot product: your push against the rail's unit arrow. For any two
arrows it is how much of one lies along the other, times the other's length:

$$\vec A\cdot\vec B = |\vec A|\,|\vec B|\cos\theta = A_1B_1 + A_2B_2$$

The first component you maximised two steps ago was $\vec A\cdot\hat e_1$ all along.

<Recall id="up-ch01-recall-along" concept="dot-product">
  <div slot="front">A 10 N push points straight across a rail. How much of it moves the cart, and why?</div>

  None. Its dot product with the rail direction is $10 \times 1 \times \cos 90° = 0$: no part
  of it lies along the rail, so the rail pushes back on all of it.
</Recall>

</Step>

## What to carry forward

- An arrow is what it is. Its components are what one grid says about it.
- You place the basis. Lay an axis along what matters and a component vanishes.
- The dot product is how much of this lies along that, and it reads the same on every grid.
