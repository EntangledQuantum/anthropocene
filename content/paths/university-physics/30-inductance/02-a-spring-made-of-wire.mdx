## Name it

This is chapter 14's cart on a spring in electrical clothes. Charge is the position, current
is the velocity, the coil is the mass and the capacitor is the spring:

$$L\,\ddot q = -\frac{q}{C} \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad m\,\ddot x = -kx, \qquad \omega = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$$

$\tfrac{q^2}{2C}$ is the spring's stored energy and $\tfrac12 LI^2$ the cart's kinetic
energy, and their sum never changes.

<Recall id="up-ch30-recall-lc-spring" concept="lc-oscillation">
  <div slot="front">In an LC loop, which part plays the mass and which the spring? Where is the energy when the current is largest?</div>

  The coil is the mass: it keeps the current going. The capacitor is the spring: it pushes
  the charge back. At peak current the capacitor is empty and all the energy is ½LI² in the
  coil, like a cart passing the middle at full speed.
</Recall>

</Step>

## What to carry forward

- **An LC loop is a mass on a spring.** The coil is the mass, the capacitor the spring, and $\omega = 1/\sqrt{LC}$.
- **The energy sloshes, it never vanishes:** all in the capacitor when the current stops, all in the coil when the plates are empty.
- **Real wire has resistance**, which drains a little every cycle, as friction does on a spring.

Next: drive the loop with an alternating voltage, and it is chapter 14's resonance again.
