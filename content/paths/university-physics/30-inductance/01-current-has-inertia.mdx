## Name it

A coil makes a voltage that fights any change in its own current:

$$\mathcal{E}_L = -L\,\frac{dI}{dt}, \qquad I(t) = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad \tau = \frac{L}{R}$$

$L$ does for current what mass does for velocity: **an inductor is inertia for current.**
Chapter 14's mass on a spring comes back in the next lesson, made of wire.

<Recall id="up-ch30-recall-inertia" concept="self-inductance">
  <div slot="front">A coil of plain copper sits in series with a lamp. What is the current the instant the switch closes, and long afterwards?</div>

  Zero at the instant: the coil's voltage $-L\,dI/dt$ cancels the battery. Long afterwards,
  the full $\mathcal{E}/R$, exactly as if the coil were not there. A coil resists changes in
  current, not current.
</Recall>

## Let it go

Open the switch on a running coil and the current cannot stop at once. It jumps the gap as
a spark, carrying off the energy the coil's field was holding.

<LetTheCoilGo
  id="up-ch30-half-the-energy"
  prompt="Close the switch, then open it when the coil holds half the energy it would hold at full current."
  explanation="At 0.35 A, 71% of the full current. The field's energy is ½LI², so it goes as the square of the current: at half the current the field holds only a quarter. Here that is 1.5 J of the 3.0 J it holds at 0.5 A."
/>

<Recall id="up-ch30-recall-spark" concept="inductor-energy">
  <div slot="front">A coil carrying current has its circuit broken. Where does its energy go, and how much is there?</div>

  $\tfrac12 LI^2$, stored in its magnetic field. The current cannot stop instantly, so the
  voltage across the gap climbs until the air breaks down, and the energy leaves as a spark.
  A car's ignition coil works exactly this way.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **A coil resists change in current, not current.** It starts at zero and ends at $\mathcal{E}/R$.
- **$\tau = L/R$:** more coil is slower, more resistance is faster.
- **The energy is $\tfrac12 LI^2$ in the field**, and it has to come out somewhere.

Next: give that energy a capacitor to go into instead of a spark, and it comes back.
