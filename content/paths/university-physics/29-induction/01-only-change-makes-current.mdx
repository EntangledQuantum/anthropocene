## Name it

The shading in the coil is the **flux**: the field threading it, times the area it threads. Each
drawn line carries the same flux, so the lines through the coil count it. The needle reads how
fast that count changes:

$$\mathcal E = -N\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}, \qquad \Phi_B = \int \vec B\cdot d\vec A$$

<Recall id="up-ch29-recall-faraday" concept="faradays-law">
  <div slot="front">One coil has a weak magnet moving through it; another has a very strong magnet sitting still inside. Which drives more current?</div>

  The moving weak one. EMF is the *rate of change* of flux. A still magnet, however strong,
  drives none.
</Recall>

</Step>

## Three handles on a flux

$\Phi_B = BA\cos\theta$: a field, an area, and the angle between the field and the loop's face.

  <div slot="front">A loop is tilted edge-on in a steady field. The field has not changed. Has the flux?</div>

  Yes, it has fallen to zero. Flux is field times the area it threads *face-on*: $BA\cos\theta$.

## What to carry forward

- **Current comes from change.** A flux that sits still drives nothing, however large.
- **Flux has three handles:** the field, the area and the angle. Change any of them and the needle moves.
