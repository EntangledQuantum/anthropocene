  <div slot="front">A planet is three times farther from the Sun at its farthest point than at its closest. How do its speeds at those two points compare?</div>

  Three times slower when farthest. At both points the velocity is at right angles to the

## Name it

With the zero of potential energy at infinity, energy per kilogram is

$$\frac{E}{m} = \tfrac12 v^2 - \frac{GM}{r}$$

Negative is bound and zero is the edge, so $v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{2GM/r} = \sqrt2\,v_{\text{circ}}$:
a circle carries half the kinetic energy that escape needs.

<Recall id="up-ch13-recall-escape" concept="escape-speed">
  <div slot="front">Circular speed just above the Moon's surface is 1.68 km/s. What is escape speed there?</div>

  About 2.38 km/s, which is √2 × 1.68. Escape needs twice the kinetic energy of a circle at the
  same place, and twice the energy is √2 times the speed.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Recall id="up-ch13-recall-chase" concept="keplers-third-law">
  <div slot="front">To catch a station ahead of you in the same circular orbit, which way do you fire?</div>

  Backward. You drop into a smaller orbit with a shorter period, gain on the station each lap,
  then fire forward to rise back. T² ∝ a³: smaller orbits are quicker.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **Equal areas in equal times**: fast near the centre, slow far away.
- **E = ½v² − GM/r decides the fate**: negative comes back; zero escapes, at √2 times circular speed.
- **Bigger orbits are slower and longer**: T² ∝ a³.
