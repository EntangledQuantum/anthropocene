## One second of falling and missing

Zoom in on the first second of a shot at 400 km. Gravity pulls the ball down 4.35 m in that
second, whatever its sideways speed.

<OneSecondOfOrbit
  id="up-ch13-one-second"
  prompt="Drag the ball sideways until, after one second, it has lost no height."
  start={5}
  explanation="7.67 km/s. In one second the ball goes 7.67 km sideways and falls 4.35 m, and over 7.67 km the Earth's curve drops exactly 4.35 m below a straight line. The fall and the curve match second after second, so the height never changes: a circular orbit."
/>

</Step>

<Step>

## Name it

A circle drops $d^2/2r$ below a straight line in a distance $d$; the ball falls $\tfrac12 g t^2$.
With $d = vt$ the two match only when

$$v^2 = g\,r = \frac{GM}{r} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad v_{\text{circ}} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$$

Gravity is supplying exactly the $v^2/r$ a circle needs, and gravity itself is $g = GM/r^2$:
9.82 m/s² at the ground, 8.69 m/s² at 400 km.

<Recall id="up-ch13-recall-float" concept="circular-orbit">
  <div slot="front">Gravity at the space station is about 90% of its value on the ground. So why do the astronauts float?</div>

  They and the station fall together, with nothing pushing on them. Both fall toward the Earth
  at 8.7 m/s², and both move sideways at 7.7 km/s, so the ground curves away as fast as they fall.
</Recall>

</Step>

<Step>

## The Moon is falling too

The Moon is 60 times farther from the Earth's centre than you are, so the same gravity is
60² = 3,600 times weaker there.

/>

<Recall id="up-ch13-recall-inverse-square" concept="universal-gravitation">
  <div slot="front">You move from the ground to twice as far from the Earth's centre. What happens to gravity's pull on you?</div>

  It drops to a quarter. Gravity falls as 1/r², with r measured from the centre, so the station's
  400 km barely matters (89%) but another 6,371 km cuts it to 25%.
</Recall>

## What to carry forward

- **An orbit is free fall** with enough sideways speed that the ground curves away as fast as you fall.
- **Gravity weakens as 1/r² from the centre**: 89% at the station, 1/3600 at the Moon, never zero.
- **A circle at radius r needs v = √(GM/r)**: 7.67 km/s at 400 km.

Next: fire faster than that, and the height stops being constant. How fast is gone for good?
